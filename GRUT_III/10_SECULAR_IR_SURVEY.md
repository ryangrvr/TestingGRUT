# 10 — Secular infrared growth in de Sitter: do the resummations disagree? (O5 item 3)

**Why this file exists.** `05` O5 item 3 said: if the literature's resummations of secular infrared growth agree, the
last opening closes; if they disagree, the size of their disagreement is the exact room a "responsive" correction could
occupy. This is the check, done before spending any computation. Method: four independent reading agents fetched the
papers (full text where arXiv allowed; abstracts otherwise), quoted them at equation level, and a fifth synthesised;
the author then read the group reports and corrected the synthesis where the harness had truncated its inputs (the
synthesis agent received only the two scalar reports intact; the graviton and item-1 sections below therefore combine
its own reading with the two group reports it did not receive). Sources and what was actually read are listed at the
end. Nothing here is a GRUT result; it is the state of the conventional literature.

**Verdicts in one paragraph.** *Scalars:* no opening — every method that reaches the light/massless regime (stochastic,
leading-log diagrammatics, wavefunction, static-patch, soft-dS EFT, Euclidean zero mode) lands on the same Fokker–Planck
equation and equilibrium, three methods agree at NLO, and the one historical prefactor discrepancy was resolved in favour
of the stochastic value. *Gravitons:* a live disagreement, but not between competing resummations (none exists for
graviton loops): the dispute is whether fixed-gauge secular logarithms survive in gauge-invariant observables, and the two
camps do not compute the same observable. That is a question about the definition of observables in perturbative quantum
gravity on de Sitter; none of P0–P5 speaks to it. *Item 1:* the inputs are textbook (Abbott–Wise 1984; Lucchin–Matarrese
1985), the dS detector response is Garbrecht–Prokopec 2004, the O(ε) zero-mode Fokker–Planck coefficients are
Cho–Kim–Kitamoto 2015; only the O(ε) worldline KMS defect of the massless sector is unpublished, and it is a small
conventional calculation. The one decisive cheap computation was `v13`; its rev.-1 result (no horizon temperature reproduces the published coefficients) was withdrawn as an inference in rev. 2 — the coefficient depends on the coarse-graining scale — and the closure of items 1 and 3 rests on P1 and conventional QFT; see `05` O5 and `RESPONSE_TO_REVIEWS.md` R18–R19.

### Results and agreement at leading log

Every method that reaches the light/massless regime lands on the same object: the Starobinsky Fokker–Planck equation with noise H³/8π² and drift 1/3H, and its equilibrium P_eq ∝ exp(−8π²V/3H⁴).

- Starobinsky–Yokoyama (Eq. 21): "any solution (14) asymptotically approaches the static equilibrium solution ρ₁eq(φ) = N⁻¹ exp(−8π²V(φ)/(3H⁴))"; for λφ⁴/4, ⟨φ²⟩ ≈ 0.132 H²/√λ (Eq. 23), relaxation Λ₁ ≈ 1.36859 √(λ/24π²) H (Eq. 47).
- Tsamis–Woodard (Sec. 7): "Starobinskii has long argued that his formalism of stochastic inflation recovers the leading infrared logarithms for scalar fields with non-derivative interactions. We have confirmed this using the infrared-truncated Yang–Feldman equation"; their Eq. (114) gives the perturbative series ⟨φ²⟩ = (H² ln a/4π²)(1 − λ ln²a/36π² + …), "in precise agreement with detailed and highly non-trivial calculations of the same quantities using quantum field theory".
- Gorbenko–Senatore (Eq. 5.5–5.6): "This equation agrees with the one obtained by Starobinsky [14, 23]. However, now we have derived this equation rigorously with control over all the approximations that were made."
- Mirbabayi 2019 (abstract): "we find the evolution to be Markovian, governed by the same Fokker-Planck equation that arises when the theory is studied in the inflationary setup."
- Cohen–Green (Sec. 5.2): Eq. (5.32) "is equivalent to the equations for stochastic inflation"; "stochastic inflation should not be thought of as a special technique … it is instead simply what falls out of the SdSET as the result of a textbook EFT analysis."
- Baumgart–Sundrum (abstract): "This rigorously leads to the 'Stochastic Inflation' ansatz developed by Starobinsky to cure the scalar infrared pathology nonperturbatively."
- Beneke–Moch (abstract): "the value of the long-wavelength mode two-point function in Euclidean de Sitter space agrees at leading order with the stochastic treatment in Lorentzian de Sitter space, in any number of dimensions."
- Burgess et al. 2015 (abstract): "the evolution of the diagonal density-matrix elements quickly approach the Fokker-Planck equation of Starobinsky's stochastic inflationary picture."
- Beneke–Hager–Sanfilippo 2026 (Sec. 1): the stochastic approach "successfully sums leading logarithmic IR and secular terms of correlation functions to all orders in the coupling".

Beyond leading log the overlap is also consistent. The NLO correction (an effective-potential/drift shift removable by a field redefinition, V_eff = V + c_UV V″ + ½V′² in Mirbabayi's form) is obtained independently by Gorbenko–Senatore (Eq. 8.13–8.18), Mirbabayi 2020 ("The result (4.7) … agrees with the finding of [11]" = Gorbenko–Senatore) and SdSET (Cohen et al. 2021, Sec. 7: "This result agrees with Refs. [30, 32, 34]"). At NNLO the first non-Gaussian noise term (Kramers–Moyal d₀ = λ/192π², "basis independent") is computed in SdSET; the two-loop diffusion correction D = (H³/8π²)[1 + (κ/24π²)(L_µ² + L_µ − 8/3 + …)] of 2604.14283 is µ-dependent and "cancelled in observables by other NNLO effects that are not yet completely known". That is incompleteness, not disagreement.

### Genuine disagreements, past and present

1. **Prefactor of the dynamical mass (2011–2012), resolved.** Beneke–Moch, Sec. 1: "while both formalisms agree on the parametric size √λ H² of the dynamical mass squared, the two exact results from [2, 5] disagree on the numerical prefactor" (SY vs Garbrecht–Rigopoulos CTP Schwinger–Dyson). Their Euclidean zero-mode computation sides with SY: "the long-wavelength two-point functions (and therefore 'dynamical masses') are the same, as claimed" (Sec. 7).

2. **Controllability of the massless limit (2010), since answered.** Burgess–Holman–Leblond–Shandera: "We regard it to be an open question whether the perturbative problems that arise here can themselves be resummed in a controllable fashion"; the stochastic approach "is argued to capture (and resum) the leading logs … [but] it is not yet clear what combination of small parameters control the approximations made in its derivation" (Sec. 4). Burgess et al. 2015 repeat the caution: their Gaussian derivation "is unable to directly verify the validity of Starobinsky's use of this equation in the late-time limit to yield very non-gaussian probability distributions" (Sec. 5.2). The two reports read this differently: group A lists it as a live "disagreement about whether the SY/TW leading-log resummation is a controlled approximation in the massless limit"; group B says there is "no genuine, unresolved disagreement". Chronology reconciles them. The 2019–2021 derivations (Gorbenko–Senatore's explicit control parameters; Baumgart–Sundrum's all-orders LL theorem; Mirbabayi's Markovianity proof; SdSET power counting showing corrections "are limited to higher orders in the marginal coupling") supply exactly what Burgess et al. asked for. No post-2019 paper in either report disputes this.

3. **Euclidean vs Lorentzian.** Gorbenko–Senatore on Rajaraman/Beneke–Moch zero-mode treatments: "this treatment is not sufficient to make the correlation functions well behaved once they are rotated back to Lorentzian space … one still has IR (specifically secular) divergences". Beneke–Hager–Sanfilippo 2026 concede the point: "these secular divergences originate from a continuous set of IR modes characterised by kη → 0, and there is currently no way of resumming these using analytic continuation from Euclidean space." This is a limitation of one method, agreed by both sides, not a conflicting prediction.

4. **Remaining open technicalities (not disagreements).** Baumgart–Sundrum: NLL resummability "not yet definitively established" (since addressed at NLO by GS/Mirbabayi/SdSET). Beneke et al. 2026: a general proof that RG-flow = time-flow in SdSET "is still missing". Cohen–Green: "the lack of a well-defined observable in the absence of a fixed boundary" (eternal inflation). Marolf–Morrison and Hollands are massive-field results, explicitly not applicable ("our method would definitely fail for massless scalar fields", Hollands).

**Net for scalars:** no two methods that compute the same late-time quantity disagree on it. The leading-log equilibrium, the relaxation exponents (SY numerics; NLO corrections in Mirbabayi 2020 and GS), and the NLO drift are agreed across stochastic, diagrammatic, wavefunction, static-patch, EFT and Euclidean treatments. The one historical numerical discrepancy was resolved in favour of the stochastic value.

## 2. Gravitons: the secular-growth debate

Here "resummations disagree" is the wrong frame: there is as yet no complete leading-log resummation for graviton loops to compare. What exists is a two-decade disagreement over whether the large logarithms found in fixed-gauge graviton-loop computations are physical.

**Position A (Tsamis–Woodard and collaborators): the logs are physical.**
- 0708.2004 (abstract, ABSTRACT_ONLY): "gauge dependence does not render a Green's function devoid of physical content … the same secular corrections appear in observable quantities in quantum gravity … the prospects seem good for deriving a simple stochastic formulation of quantum gravity in which the leading secular effects can be summed".
- Tan–Wang–Woodard 2021 (2107.13905, abstract): one-graviton-loop corrections to gravitational radiation "are enhanced by the square of the number of inflationary e-foldings. One consequence is that perturbation theory breaks down for a very long epoch of primordial inflation."
- Tan–Wang–Woodard 2022 (2206.11467, FULLTEXT, closing section): "gravitons induce up to three large logarithms, whereas a loop of massless, minimally coupled scalars produces only a single large logarithm"; and, candidly, "The large logarithms we have found all derive from terms which carry factors of H², and their gauge dependence is not known … we expect that the elimination of gauge dependence will not eliminate large graviton logarithms but might change their numerical coefficients."
- Glavan–Miao–Prokopec–Woodard 2024 (2402.05452, FULLTEXT, Sec. 6): for the massless-scalar exchange potential, "The fact that [the gauge-independent α₂R coefficient] is nonzero contradicts the hypothesis that graviton-induced logarithms are a gauge artifact." But: "We will not feel completely confident until the gauge independence of the final result has been confirmed", since the de Sitter Donoghue identities were obtained by "minimal coupling (which is a guess)". Follow-ups 2602.07908 and 2609.10365 (ABSTRACT_ONLY) report the cancellation in a one-parameter gauge family once external-leg corrections are included.
- Miao–Tsamis–Woodard 2024 (2405.01024, FULLTEXT, Sec. 6): a leading-log resummation exists for *scalar* loops acting on gravity; for graviton loops "it is inevitable that the graviton-induced stress tensor will not be conserved … we do not expect that a local [extension] can be found", and "1-loop corrections to the graviton mode function … will involve stochastic fluctuations … in addition to renormalization group effects." The resummation is a plan, not a result.

**Position B (Garriga–Tanaka; Tanaka–Urakawa; Senatore–Zaldarriaga; Higuchi–Marolf–Morrison; Fröb et al.): the secular logs are gauge/observable artefacts in properly defined quantities.**
- Garriga–Tanaka 2007 (0706.0295, abstract): "the tadpole correction to the local expansion rate … is not gauge invariant. On the other hand, we construct a gauge invariant operator which measures the renormalized curvature scalar smeared over an arbitrary window function, and we find that there is no secular screening of this quantity (to all orders in perturbation theory)."
- Tanaka–Urakawa 2013/2014 (1301.3088, 1402.2076, abstracts): "as long as we consider a gauge invariant quantity in the local universe, we encounter neither IR divergence nor secular growth" for graviton loops in general single-field inflation, with the Euclidean vacuum, "up to a sufficiently high order in perturbation".
- Senatore–Zaldarriaga 2012 (1210.6048, abstract): "ζ-correlators are time-independent at large distances at all-loop level in single clock inflation."
- Higuchi–Marolf–Morrison 2011 (1107.2712, abstract): the graviton IR divergence in TT synchronous gauge "can be removed by adding a linearized diffeomorphism to each mode function; i.e., by an explicit change of gauge. It follows that the graviton vacuum state is well-defined and de Sitter invariant."
- Fröb–Lima and collaborators (1711.08470, 2303.16218, 2109.09753, abstracts) build relational/gauge-invariant observables; 2303.16218 shows the gauge-invariant synchronous two-point function "only contains the physical tensor modes and it is thus positive".

**What is gauge-dependent and what is claimed physical.** Both camps now agree that fixed-gauge graviton Green's functions and the tadpole/expansion-rate "screening" diagnostic are gauge-dependent (Woodard's own 2024 paper opens with this). The live question is narrower: do large logarithms survive in a specific class of observables, namely quantum-corrected effective field equations for exchange potentials and mode functions, once source and observer correlations are included? Position A says yes (2402.05452, 2602.07908), with a stated caveat on the de Sitter Donoghue identities. Position B's no-secular-growth theorems concern different observables (smeared curvature scalar; local gauge-invariant ζ and tensor correlators in single-field inflation). I found no paper in which the two camps compute the *same* gauge-invariant observable and get different answers; the disagreement is partly about which quantities count as observables. Woodard's "Comes of age" review (1407.4748) and the Position B papers do not cite each other as refuted.

**Latest statements (2024–2026).** Position A's gauge-independence program is explicitly incomplete by its own account, and no stochastic/leading-log resummation for graviton loops exists. Fröb–Roura–Verdaguer 2012 (1205.3097, abstract) add a further caution relevant to any "observable" claim: with one-loop conformal-matter corrections, the tensor two-point function is a well-defined distribution "for arbitrary time separations" but "the power spectrum is strictly speaking ill-defined when loop corrections are included" (equal-time limit divergent). The record's item 2 already uses this paper as its interacting baseline.

**Does any observable settle it?** Not at present. Tan–Wang–Woodard 2021 say the one-loop tensor-spectrum correction "might be observable, in the far future, after the full development of 21 cm cosmology"; that claim depends on the as-yet-unconfirmed gauge independence of the ln²a term and on a well-defined loop-corrected spectrum (contested by FRV 2012 for matter loops). Position B's theorems are statements of absence and are not tied to a measurable signature either.


### From the graviton reading report (not seen by the synthesis agent)

**What is disputed (live).**
A. Whether graviton-LOOP large logarithms in de Sitter (graviton mode function ~ ln^2 a, Newtonian potential ~ ln^2 a and ln(Hr), scalar/photon/fermion field-strength growth: Tan–Tsamis–Woodard 2107.13905, 2206.11467; Glavan et al. 2112.00959) are physical or gauge artifacts. All were computed in the single 'simplest' de Sitter-breaking gauge. Woodard 2501.05077 openly concedes: 'loop corrections which involve graviton propagators do depend on the scheme by which the graviton gauge is fixed and this has led to a debate about the reality of the large logarithms'; the one check in a second (de Sitter-invariant) gauge family gave the same sign and form but a DIFFERENT coefficient. The Woodard camp's answer (Glavan–Miao–Prokopec–Woodard 2402.05452, 2602.07908, 2609.10365) is an S-matrix-inspired diagram combination with source/observer corrections: the log in the scalar exchange potential survives with changed coefficient ('The fact that it is nonzero contradicts the hypothesis that graviton-induced logarithms are a gauge artifact'), and in 2026 they report cancellation of one-parameter gauge dependence — but only after adding secular external-leg corrections, and they themselves wrote in 2024 that the de Sitter Donoghue identities are 'a guess' and 'We will not feel completely confident until the gauge independence of the final result has been confirmed'. Miao–Tsamis–Woodard 2409.12003 still states 'The gauge fixing dependence of all results is obviously a serious physical issue that must be addressed and resolved', while asserting 'in all cases analyzed so far the leading infrared effect is not a gauge artifact' (citing only their own work).
B. The opposing view that graviton secular growth in local-universe invariants is a gauge/large-gauge artifact: Tanaka–Urakawa 1402.2076 ('as long as we consider a gauge invariant quantity in the local universe, we encounter neither IR divergence nor secular growth'), Garriga–Tanaka's all-orders no-screening of <R_ren>_W, Giddings–Sloth (IR growth absorbable into coordinate change for local observables), and the Marolf–Morrison/Hollands IR-stability and no-hair results for the Hartle–Hawking state (massive fields; conjectured to extend to higher spin). These have never been reconciled diagram-by-diagram with the Woodard-camp results; Woodard 2025 lists them as [104–114] 'the debate' and neither side has produced a calculation the other accepts as refuting it.
C. A third position, from the Froeb school using fully relational invariant observables: a GENUINE secular graviton-loop effect exists at one loop in non-de-Sitter (constant-epsilon) inflation — <H_ren> = H[1 + (21/128 pi^2) kappa^2 H^2 epsilon ln a] (Froeb 1806.11124 eq. 120; Lima 2007.04995 for finite second slow-roll parameter) — but with the OPPOSITE net sign to Tsamis–Woodard (acceleration toward de Sitter, although the graviton mutual-attraction piece does slow expansion 'as it was proposed more than 25 years ago'), and vanishing in exact de Sitter. Froeb also explicitly disputes Garriga–Tanaka's theorem ('seemingly in contradiction... for their result to hold one needs a scalar W(x) that is independent of the metric perturbations, which seems impossible to obtain without adding additional matter fields'). So the invariant-observable camp says: secular graviton effects CAN be physical, but the TW screening sign/mechanism is not what the invariant one-loop calculation shows, and the de Sitter two-loop question is open ('It would be valuable to extend the above results to two-loop corrections, where also in pure de Sitter space a non-trivial result is expected').
D. Methodological disagreement on what counts as gauge independent: Woodard's group ('combining bits and pieces of gauge-dependent Green's functions ... based on the flat space S-matrix') vs the relational/invariant-operator approach of Brunetti–Fredenhagen–Hack–Pinamonti–Rejzner, Froeb, Lima, Tanaka–Urakawa; 2402.05452 states 'Opinions differ on how to deal with gauge dependence.' No paper has yet shown the two prescriptions agree on any de Sitter graviton-loop logarithm, and Froeb 1706.01891 shows that different invariant constructions (geodesic distance vs relational) give different log structures ('double logarithms at one-loop order').

**What would settle it.** (i) The two-loop expectation value of a fully gauge-invariant (relational, quasi-local, causal) expansion-rate observable in pure de Sitter gravity, computed with dimensional regularization — this is the quantity both camps name (MTW 1702.05694: 'the lowest secular back-reaction should be a 2-loop effect'; Froeb 1806.11124: 'at two-loop corrections ... also in pure de Sitter space a non-trivial result is expected'). A ln a or ln^2 a term there with a definite sign would decide screening vs no screening; zero would vindicate Garriga–Tanaka/Tanaka–Urakawa. (ii) Short of that, the decisive one-loop test is already framed by both sides: compute the one-graviton-loop correction to the invariant (relational) graviton two-point function / tensor power spectrum or to the invariant Newtonian potential on de Sitter in a general two-parameter covariant gauge family and check whether Tan–Tsamis–Woodard's ln^2 a coefficient is reproduced; the Woodard group's own stated completion criterion is the full two-parameter (alpha, beta) cancellation (2501.05077 Sec. 5.3), which as of 2026 has been done only for one parameter (2602.07908). Agreement between the S-matrix-style and relational constructions on that coefficient would close the question; disagreement would locate the artifact. Observationally the only proposed signature is the one-loop ln^2(a) correction to the primordial tensor spectrum via future 21 cm measurements (2107.13905), far beyond current reach. Bottom line for GRUT III item 3: the literature's resummations do NOT yet agree — the stochastic/RG leading-log program (MTW 2409.12003, 2507.04308) presupposes physical secular graviton logs whose gauge independence its own authors describe as incompletely established, while invariant-observable and local-gauge-invariance analyses either find zero (de Sitter, one loop; Tanaka–Urakawa all local invariants) or a secular effect of opposite sign (Froeb, slow-roll). This is a genuine, open window, not a closed one.

## 3. Is item 1 already published?

Largely yes, in three pieces; one piece is not cleanly in the literature as far as I found.

- **Massless modes in power-law inflation**: Abbott–Wise 1984 (Nucl. Phys. B244, 541; 898 INSPIRE citations) and Lucchin–Matarrese 1985 (Phys. Rev. D32, 1316; 1031 citations). Metadata confirmed only; no arXiv text (NOT READ). Textbook.
- **Comoving-detector response for the massless minimally coupled field in dS**: Garbrecht–Prokopec 2004 (gr-qc/0404058, FULLTEXT). Their Eq. (30) gives the response for m_φ = 0: dF/dt = (ΔE/2π)[1 + H²/ΔE²] /(e^{2πΔE/H} − 1) + (H²/2π) δ(ΔE)[ln a − ¼ + ln 2]; the dS-breaking ln a term "vanishes for all ΔE ≠ 0, such that … the detector is insensitive to the breaking of de Sitter invariance by the propagator." The response is not Planckian in form but obeys detailed balance (their Eq. 49, 52–53) at T_H = H/2π: "the energy levels of the detector are thermally populated." They also note (Sec. V) that "the periodicity of the metric however does not coincide with the Hawking temperature is a quasi-de Sitter space considered in Ref. [20]" = Brandenberger–Kahn 1982, Phys. Lett. B119, 75 (not read), which is the closest antecedent to the record's T_det ≠ T_KH point.
- **O(ε) correction to the stochastic diffusion and drift in a constant-ε accelerating universe**: Cho–Kim–Kitamoto 2015 (1508.07877, FULLTEXT in part). Their Fokker–Planck Eq. (3.15)–(3.16): ρ̇ = ½A₀H³ ∂²ρ/∂φ² + (1/((3−ε₀)H)) ∂_φ(ρ V′), with A₀ = (2−2ε₀)^{2ν₀−3} Γ²(ν₀)(1−ε₀)³/π³; generalized to time-dependent ε in Eqs. (5.6)–(5.9). At ε = 0 this is H³/8π² and 1/3H. If ν = 3/2 + ε/(1−ε) as in the record's lemma, my expansion (not quoted from the paper; to be checked) gives ½A H³ = (H³/8π²)[1 + (1 − 2γ_E − 2 ln 2) ε + O(ε²)] ≈ (H³/8π²)(1 − 1.54 ε) (corrected after `v13`: the earlier draft omitted the −2 ln 2; this is the sharp-window σ = 1 value), i.e. a multiplicative O(ε) correction *beyond* the H → H(t) replacement the record anticipates ("expected: D(t) = H(t)³/4π²"). They further cite Prokopec [16] for an analytic final state when ε is constant and couplings dimensionless. Kitamoto 2018 (1811.01830) extend the resummation to derivative interactions (nonlinear sigma models), the scalar proxy for gravity's interactions; Miao–Tsamis–Woodard 2021 (2110.08715) do the same with an added RG piece.
- **Not found**: an explicit computation of the stationary-part KMS temperature (or KMS defect) of the comoving worldline two-time function of the massless minimally coupled field in power-law FRW at O(ε), with the power-law t(η). INSPIRE searches for Unruh–DeWitt detectors in power-law/FRW backgrounds returned nothing on point. Brandenberger–Kahn 1982 may contain the periodicity statement; it should be read before any claim of novelty.

So item 1's diffusion-constant part is published (and the published answer differs at O(ε) from the record's stated expectation), its dS detector part is published, and only the O(ε) worldline KMS defect of the massless sector in power-law FRW appears to be open, and that is a small, conventional calculation.


### From the item-1 reading report (not seen by the synthesis agent)

Short verdict: the INPUTS to O5 item 1 are fully in print; the TARGET of item 1 is not. Roughly the 'easy half' is published.

Published (agreements across sources):
1. Massless minimally coupled and tensor mode functions / spectra in power-law inflation a ∝ t^p: textbook since Abbott-Wise 1984 (tensors) and Lucchin-Matarrese 1985 (scalar perturbations); both abstracts confirm scope (full texts not reachable from this container, so the equations were not re-read). 2. ## 4. Verdict for GRUT III

**Scalar sector: no opening.** The condition O5-3 sets ("if they agree, the opening closes") is met. Stochastic, diagrammatic, wavefunction, static-patch, soft-EFT and Euclidean methods agree at leading log on the same Fokker–Planck equation and equilibrium; three independent methods agree at NLO; NNLO is partially computed and internally consistent so far; the only historical numerical discrepancy (dynamical-mass prefactor) was resolved in favour of the stochastic value; the 2010 controllability objection was answered by the 2019–2021 rigorous derivations. There is no ΔW(t,t′) that conventional theory leaves undetermined for a light scalar in dS, and the quasi-dS extension at O(ε) is also published (Cho–Kim–Kitamoto [arXiv:1508.07877; the 2018 derivative-interaction paper 1811.01830 is Kitamoto alone]). A GRUT "restriction" here could only reproduce known results.

**Graviton sector: unresolved, and it is a quantum-gravity question, not a GRUT one.** There are no competing graviton resummations to measure a gap between; there is a dispute over whether fixed-gauge secular logarithms survive in gauge-invariant observables, with Position A's gauge-independence program self-described as not yet confirmed and Position B's no-growth theorems applying to differently defined observables. The disagreement is about the definition of observables in perturbative quantum gravity on dS (relational observables, source/observer correlations, Donoghue identities on curved backgrounds). None of P0–P5 speaks to that; GRUT III, by its own `05` framing ("gravity from local Rindler horizons", D3 = Einstein equations), takes the gravitational field as classical with a thermodynamic reading, and the contested logs are one-graviton-loop effects. Should Position A's resummation program succeed, it will be a stochastic Langevin system for the graviton zero mode analogous to the scalar one; a GRUT statement could then at most be a KMS constraint on its coefficients, which (by the scalar precedent) conventional theory will already fix.

**One most informative next computation.** Rather than O5 item 1 as written, compute whether P1's KMS reading at T = (ä/ȧ)/2π fixes the O(ε) Fokker–Planck coefficients of the massless minimally coupled zero mode in power-law FRW, and compare with the published conventional answer, Cho–Kim–Kitamoto Eq. (3.16): noise ½AH³ with A = (2−2ε)^{2ν−3}Γ²(ν)(1−ε)³/π³ and drift 1/((3−ε)H). This is cheap (it reuses the `v12` 12.4 lemma, with the power-law t(η)), it has a published baseline to be right or wrong against, and it is decisive either way: agreement closes O5 items 1 and 3 together and leaves O5 item 2 (one-loop conformal-matter tensor two-time function, extending FRV 2012) as the last conventional baseline; disagreement would be the first place GRUT III says something conventional theory does not, in a sector where the conventional answer is settled enough to adjudicate. (Audit, `11`: less settled than this says — the published drift 1/((3−ε)H) is a leading-IR approximation, the σ = 1 noise a sharp-window value; the σ-free invariant is the zero-frequency noise strength, `v13` 13.5.) If that agrees, the honest closing line of `05` O5 applies: GRUT III is GR + QFT with a thermodynamic reading, and the graviton infrared is left to quantum gravity.

**Done and restated (`v13` rev. 1 → rev. 2).** Rev. 1 took the published σ = 1 coefficients and found that the implied zero-mode temperature matches no horizon temperature (c_X = 0.4685). The reviewer showed that the O(ε) coefficient depends on the coarse-graining scale k = σaH: rev. 2 derives A₀(σ) = A₀(1)σ^{−2ε/(1−ε)} from the mode function, hence c_X(σ) = 0.4685 + ½ ln σ (0 at σ = 0.39, 1/2 at σ = 1.07, 1 at σ = 2.9, −∞ as σ → 0). The coefficient is not a physical invariant and cannot test horizon temperatures; rev. 1's inference is withdrawn. Items 1 and 3 still close, on P1 (no geometric modular flow → no P1 temperature) and on conventional QFT fixing the σ-independent correlators. The σ-independent test is the comoving detector's KMS ratio at ω ≠ 0 (`v14`).
