## 3. Theorem and proof

**Class 𝓗.** System `q ∈ ℝⁿ`, momenta `p`, `H_S = ½pᵀM⁻¹p + V(q)` with `V` smooth and confining (any
nonlinearity). Bath: harmonic modes `(x_j, p_j)`, `j` in a finite set. Coupling: linear in `x_j` and in
`q`, with counterterm (as in §2). **Preparation hypothesis (P):** given the system initial state
`(q₀, p₀)`, the free-force process `F` defined below has a law `𝒫` that does not depend on `(q₀, p₀)`.

**Theorem.** Let A be the Hamiltonian system with bath initial states drawn per (P). Let B be the GLE

`M q̈ = −∇V(q) − ∫₀ᵗ γ(t−s) q̇(s) ds + F(t)`, `γ(t) = Σ_j c_j c_jᵀ/ω_j² cos ω_j t`,

driven by an exogenous process `F ~ 𝒫`, independent of `(q₀, p₀)`. Then for every `(q₀, p₀)` the laws
of the system path `q(·)` on any `[0, t_max]` under A and under B coincide. In particular every reduced
statistic agrees: mean responses, all moments, multi-time correlations, the C-B discriminator.

**Proof.** The bath obeys `ẍ_j = −ω_j²x_j + c_jᵀq(t)`, a linear ODE driven by the system path.
Variation of constants plus one integration by parts give

`x_j(t) − c_jᵀq(t)/ω_j² = [x_j(0) − c_jᵀq₀/ω_j²] cos ω_j t + p_j(0)/ω_j sin ω_j t − (1/ω_j²)∫₀ᵗ cos ω_j(t−s) c_jᵀq̇(s) ds.`

The force on the system is `Σ_j c_j (x_j − c_jᵀq/ω_j²) = F(t) − ∫₀ᵗ γ(t−s)q̇(s) ds`, with

`F(t) = Σ_j c_j {[x_j(0) − c_jᵀq₀/ω_j²] cos ω_j t + p_j(0)/ω_j sin ω_j t}`,

a function of the bath's initial data alone. So the system path under A solves the GLE with this
`F`. For continuous `F` the GLE (a Volterra equation with locally Lipschitz drift) has a unique local
solution. It is global because confining `V` plus positive-type `γ` give an energy bound. So
`q(·) = Φ(q₀, p₀, F)` for one measurable map `Φ` that is **the same in A and B**. Under (P),
`Law_A(q(·) | q₀, p₀) = Φ(q₀, p₀, ·)#𝒫 = Law_B(q(·) | q₀, p₀)`. ∎

A continuum bath is the limit of finite baths applied identically to A and B; equivalently, run the
same argument with `F` any process whose law satisfies (P).

**Corollaries.**

- **(i) Thermal shifted-Gibbs preparation:** `F` is Gaussian, mean 0, covariance `Tγ(t−s)`. A Gaussian
  law is fixed by its first two moments, so **matched covariance suffices**. Checked exactly
  (C2): `E q⁽ⁿ⁾(0)` agrees between A and B for every `n ≤ 8` (two modes) and `n ≤ 12` (one mode), as
  exact rationals in `β, T, a`.
- **(ii) Product preparation** (bath Gibbs about `x = 0`, ignoring `q₀`): (P) fails. `F` acquires a
  deterministic, preparation-dependent **initial slip** `−γ(t)q₀`. A and the shift-matched B differ
  at `n = 2, 4, …`, and A equals B plus that deterministic slip term **exactly** (C2). Whether the slip
  is filed under "noise" or "memory" is a bookkeeping convention, not an observable.
- **(iii) Deterministic microstate:** `𝒫` is a Dirac mass on one path. The reduced process is not
  stochastic, and B "matches" it with the same Dirac law. Pathwise check (C1, one bath microstate,
  40 modes): the full Hamiltonian `q(t)` and the GLE driven by the computed `F` agree to `5·10⁻⁶`
  (Drude), `1.4·10⁻⁶` (two-exponential, CM), `4·10⁻⁶` (resonant, **non-CM**). This is the `O(dt²)`
  error of the Volterra integrator. **CM vs non-CM is irrelevant to identifiability.**
- **(iv) Realizability converse (standard, classical):** any exogenous pair (`γ` of positive cosine
  type, `F` stationary Gaussian with spectral density `S_F` supported where `J > 0`) has a harmonic
  bath parent with **mode-dependent temperatures** `T(ω) ∝ S_F(ω)/(J(ω)/ω)`. FDT (one temperature) is
  the special case. So "exogenous Gaussian noise + memory" and "autonomous harmonic bath" are
  interchangeable descriptions in both directions.

**What the reduced data *do* see (the cumulant ledger).**

- **Lemma (O(β), from linear-response expansion).** To first order in β,
  `S − D = −4β ∫₀ᵗ g(t−s) [3μ(s)v(s) + κ₃(s)] ds`. Here `μ, v, κ₃` are the mean, variance and third
  cumulant of the linear (β = 0) retained coordinate, and `g` is its linear response. The part **odd
  in `a`** (the C-B ± pair) depends on the forcing **covariance only**. For symmetric forcing laws
  (thermal, random-phase) `κ₃ = 0`.
- **Inside the white C-B class** (C4, exact): with extra noise cumulant rates `κ₃, κ₄`,
  `Δc₂ = −24βTa − 4βκ₃`. The `κ₃` term is even in `a` and drops out of the ± pair, so the record's
  odd-in-`a` discriminator is covariance-only. **`κ₄` first enters at `Δc₃`: `+240β²κ₄a`.**
- **Bath version** (C2): a random-phase / fixed-energy bath (same covariance, non-Gaussian) first
  differs from the thermal bath at `t¹²`: `E q⁽¹²⁾` differs by `138510·T²aβ²`, i.e. through the fourth
  cumulant at `O(β²)`. An **exogenous** random-phase process with the same law matches that bath
  exactly, at every order (C2: `R − B_R = 0`).

So the discriminator is a readout of the **forcing law's cumulant hierarchy**. It is blind to
whether that law was produced by hidden deterministic variables.

- **H2 (does it contradict HT-B?).** No. HT-B quantifies over hidden laws on the **same** state space
  (`ℝ²³`). The bath's initial data live outside it. P-17 is the enlarged-space complement HT-B
  explicitly left open.
- **H3 (is matched covariance enough?).** Only for Gaussian preparations. In general, the full law of
  `F` must match. A same-covariance, non-Gaussian bath *is* distinguishable from a Gaussian one, but
  only as a different **law**, never as a different ontology.
- **H4 (path-space encoding in disguise?).** No. The forcing history is not stored. It is produced by
  oscillators with their own energy-conserving dynamics, from a finite-dimensional initial state per
  mode. This is the "natural deterministic bath realization", distinct from the record's HB-U

- **H5 (limits).** Exact identifiability needs no limit. Only "reproduces C-B exactly" needs the

## 7. Frontier exposed (where identifiability *could* return)

Outside class 𝓗, the proof's key step fails: `F` is then no longer a function of bath initial data
alone. That happens with **anharmonic bath modes** or **coupling nonlinear in bath coordinates**,
where the bath's back-action is not linear response. It also fails for a preparation violating (P)
in a way that cannot be absorbed into the deterministic memory (here only the linear slip occurred,
and it could be absorbed). That is the only place a reduced-data distinction between hidden-bath and
exogenous noise could live. P-18 (chaotic fast variable) is the first such parent, but there,
homogenization theorems drive the limit back to Itô noise, so the expected outcome is the same
non-identifiability in the limit.
