\[

\dot\rho=-i[A,\rho]-\frac{\tau}{2}[A,[A,\rho]].}

## 2. THE DERIVATION

### Proposition 1: exact solution, existence and persistence

Let the distinct spectral values of \(A\) be \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\), with orthogonal
projectors \(P_j\). For every matrix \(X\),

\[
\Phi_t(X)=\sum_{j,k}
 e^{-i(a_j-a_k)t-\tau(a_j-a_k)^2t/2}\,P_jXP_k.
\]

**Proof.** Each block \(P_jXP_k\) is an eigenvector of the commutator with
eigenvalue \(a_j-a_k\). Solving its scalar ODE proves the formula, the initial
condition, uniqueness and the semigroup composition identity. Also

\[
\Phi_t(X)=\mathbb E\left[
 e^{-iA(t+\sqrt\tau W_t)}X e^{iA(t+\sqrt\tau W_t)}\right],
\qquad W_t\sim N(0,t).
\]

The Gaussian characteristic function gives exactly the same factors. A convex
average of unitary conjugations is CPTP, trace-preserving and unital. This
representation describes random phase; it does not posit that laboratory time
literally runs backward. All populations \(\operatorname{Tr}P_j\rho\) and
\(\operatorname{Tr}A\rho\) are conserved. For the distinct-value gap
\(g=\min_{j\ne k}|a_j-a_k|>0\),

\[
\left\|\Phi_t(\rho)-\sum_jP_j\rho P_j\right\|_{HS}
 \le e^{-\tau g^2t/2}
 \left\|\rho-\sum_jP_j\rho P_j\right\|_{HS}.
\]

Different spectral blocks are Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal, proving this bound.
When \(s=1\), the off-block part is empty and the channel is the identity.

The fixed algebra is exactly \(\{X:[A,X]=0\}\). Indeed
\(\operatorname{Re}\langle X,\mathcal L X\rangle_{HS}
=-\tau\|[A,X]\|_{HS}^2/2\); a fixed vector therefore commutes with \(A\).
The converse is immediate. Its minimal central projectors are the \(P_j\);
its dimension is \(\sum_j(\operatorname{rank}P_j)^2\). **DERIVED**, conditional
on the postulated operator and quantum kinematics.

## 3. THE RESTRICTION

With the supplied Ramsey preparation and instruments, for \(i\ne j\) set
\(\omega_{ij}=a_j-a_i\). Then

\[
z_{ij}(t):=\langle X_{ij}\rangle+i\langle Y_{ij}\rangle
 = e^{-\Gamma_{ij}t-i\omega_{ij}t},\qquad
\boxed{\Gamma_{ij}=\frac\tau2\omega_{ij}^2.}
\]

Consequently \(R_{ij,kl}=\Gamma_{ij}\omega_{kl}^2-
\Gamma_{kl}\omega_{ij}^2=0\). Phase and envelope are operationally independent
measurements. This is not an epsilon identity or a definition of the measured
decay rate. It follows for the entire candidate family, not just its fixture.
It is invariant under relabeling, simultaneous unitary conjugation, energy-zero
shift and a common change of time unit.

At fixed nondegenerate \(A\), the full ordinary dephasing baseline has rates
\(\Gamma_{ij}=\|v_i-v_j\|^2/2\), with arbitrary finite vectors \(v_i\).
Its rate cone is linearly isomorphic to the PSD cone on the centered
\((d-1)\)-dimensional subspace: for a zero-diagonal symmetric rate matrix \(G\),
\(-J G J\succeq0\), where \(J=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/d\). This is the standard
conditionally negative definite distance-matrix criterion. To see the
correspondence, write \(G_{ij}=(B_{ii}+B_{jj}-2B_{ij})/2\) with
\(B\succeq0\), \(B\mathbf1=0\); then \(-JGJ=B\). A Gram factor of \(B\)
gives the vectors. Thus the cone has an interior of dimension \(d(d-1)/2\).

The candidate restricts it to the single ray
\(G_{ij}=\tau(a_i-a_j)^2/2\). Allowing its nuisance coefficient \(\tau\) to vary
leaves dimension one, so the conditional codimension is \(d(d-1)/2-1\).
For three levels it is two. **A real conditional observable restriction is
proved.** It does not earn GRUT coupling credit because identity and readout
generation fail, and the assembled equation/restriction is pre-existing.
Residual microscopic freedom by itself is not the failure.
