## The necessity is conditional and already published

Galley, Giacomini and Selby's Theorem 1 rules out simultaneous satisfaction of
four conditions: fully nonclassical matter, a reversible joint interaction,
information flowing from matter to the gravitational system, and a classical
gravitational system [3]. Here reversibility means a physical inverse of the
joint operation; back-reaction means the source affects the gravitational
system's output. Classicality is their operational simplex state space, not
merely an approximately classical metric.

The proof is formulated for finite-dimensional general probabilistic systems
with specified composition and fixed causal structure. The paper discusses an
infinite-dimensional extension rather than supplying a complete continuum
gravitational theorem. Applying it to constrained gravitational field algebras
therefore requires care. It does not prove that all gravity must be quantum.
An observed loss of coherence in a reduced subsystem also does not establish
fundamental irreversibility: an unobserved quantum environment can cause it.

There is already constructive physics on both sides of this choice. Quantum
gravity treated as an effective field theory supplies controlled low-energy
predictions [5]; ultraviolet incompleteness is not a failure of every
low-energy calculation. Oppenheim develops stochastic classical–quantum
dynamics with linear, completely positive evolution [6]. That proposal is not
experimental validation, and its paper explicitly discusses regularization
and constraint-consistency obligations.

The published decoherence–diffusion trade-off [7] supplies a joint observable
connection under its classical–quantum, complete-positivity and time-local
assumptions. It links back-reaction to disturbance of matter and noise in the
classical sector. It is an existing restriction with a specified domain,
not a new GRUT result; the rate formulation cannot silently be extended to
arbitrary retained-memory histories.

## What remains experimentally undecided

The selected question is:

**When a source retains coherent alternatives with different mass distributions,
what gravitational response and quantum-information transfer occur, and what
disturbance accompanies that response?**

Relevant measured quantities are the calibrated gravitational response to the
source configuration, survival of source coherence, probe correlations or an
entanglement witness, and force/acceleration noise. Their joint behavior matters;
noise or decoherence alone cannot identify its origin.

Page–Geilker supplies an experimentally tested boundary for the simplest
mean-field prescription. It is not a demonstration of the gravitational field
of a source maintained in a controlled spatial superposition. No such
demonstration or gravity-mediated entanglement measurement was established by
the sources inspected for this decision.

The interpretation of a future entanglement result must specify the mediator,
matter description and possible nongravitational quantum communication.
Aziz–Howl's QFT construction [8] and Schneider–Huggett–Linnemann's
Newton–Cartan criticism [9] illustrate a live disagreement about precisely
those commitments. This assessment does not adjudicate that dispute or treat
an entanglement witness as an assumption-free certificate of quantum gravity.

**No evidence inspected forces a new GRUT mechanism beyond the strongest
conventional comparison.** The useful connection is already an independently
motivated question about gravitational response, coherence and disturbance.
A result agreeing with quantum gravitational EFT would support that description;
it would not alone derive the origin of interactions. A discrepancy would first
test the apparatus, environmental couplings and declared model assumptions.

This decision ends the comparison. It creates no candidate, new research queue,

generating-law proposal or evaluation still requires separate explicit owner
