# Closed constrained system with two physical clocks

## Assumptions and operational meaning

**Physical closure** here means that the specified degrees of freedom include the clocks, matter, memory and any readout pointers used in the calculation. There is no exterior drive, clock or observer in the model. A Hilbert-space representation, the Born rule, a single gauge constraint, a tensor factorization, access to declared measurements and a physical preparation are separate inputs. The model supplies neither gravity nor spacetime topology. Relational time is a statement about correlations between physical degrees of freedom.

Use clock spaces $A,B\simeq\mathbb C^N$, a matter qubit $S$ and a further matter space $\mathcal K$. The register $S$ is a physical property that affects one clock. In the benchmark $\mathcal K=D\otimes R$ has a data and a record qubit. The kinematic state space is
\[
\mathcal H_{\rm kin}=\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B\otimes\mathcal H_S\otimes\mathcal K .
\]
Clock readings $a,b$ live in $\mathbb Z_N$, with $N$ an odd prime and $\nu_0=1,\nu_1=2$. The relative label $r=b-\nu_s a$ is defined modulo $N$. The fixed shift and chosen unitary prefixes act as
\[
T\,|a,b,s\rangle|\psi\rangle =
|a+1,b+\nu_s,s\rangle V_{s,r,a+1}V_{s,r,a}^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle,\qquad V_{s,r,0}=I .
\]
This includes backreaction from a conserved matter degree of freedom, joint clock dependence of the interaction table, and a separate clock phase coupling. It is a finite constraint model with a programmed choice of interactions. There is no assertion that this generator is an energy, spatially local Hamiltonian or continuum gravitational constraint.

## Constraint and physical state space

The map of the clock and $S$ labels is a bijection. The matter action in each block is unitary, so $T$ is unitary. Since $s,r$ are invariant and the prefixes telescope with the specified wrap,
\[
T^N=I,\qquad P_j=\frac1N\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi ijm/N}T^m,\qquad
C=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}jP_j .
\]
These are spectral projections and a single self-adjoint first-class constraint, with $T=\exp(-2\pi i C/N)$. Finite averaging is exact for conjugation of operators because the $N$ distinct phases separate the spectral blocks of $C$. Equivalently take the compact continuous gauge group generated by integer-spectrum $C$. Its continuous group average gives the same commutant and the same $P_0$. A continuous evolution parameter, if used to express this gauge action, is redundant. The physical time readout is a register label, and a reference chart has a supplied cyclic orientation. A conversion from ticks to proper time needs a physical calibration absent here.

The physical states are supported on $\ker C$ with the induced positive inner product. The operator algebra prior to access restrictions is the commutant of $C$ represented on $\ker C$, with operators identified if their restrictions agree. To parametrize the physical space, introduce the seed Hilbert space $\mathcal L$ with coordinates $(s,r,\psi)$, and define
\[
J\,|s,r\rangle|\psi\rangle=
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}|a,r+\nu_sa,s\rangle V_{s,r,a}|\psi\rangle.
\]
Different $(s,r)$ labels are orthogonal at each $a$ and the $V$ preserve inner products. Hence $J^\dagger J=I_{\mathcal L}$, and the shift fixes its image. Conversely, a shift-invariant vector is determined by its amplitudes at $a=0$ because in each block the constraint transports the amplitude by the displayed prefixes. Thus $JJ^\dagger=P_0$. For finite $\mathcal K$, the dimension is $2N\dim\mathcal K$, equal to 40 in the registered 200-dimensional kinematic model. Every density operator $\rho$ on $\mathcal L$ gives the physical state $J\rho J^\dagger$. The constraint restricts kinematic states to a subspace but leaves the seed density unrestricted.

## Clock descriptions and conditional dynamics

The ideal clock reductions from the physical space in seed coordinates are
\[
U_A(a)=\sqrt N(\langle a|\otimes I)J,\qquad
U_B(b)=\sqrt N(\langle b|_B\otimes I_{\overline B})J .
\]
Here $\overline B$ means the remaining registers in their fixed order. On a seed $(s,r,\psi)$ the first map sends the remaining clock to $b=r+\nu_s a$ and applies $V_{s,r,a}$ to $\psi$. The second sends the remaining clock to $a=a_*(s,r,b)=\nu_s^{-1}(b-r)$ and applies $V_{s,r,a_*}$. Both are unitary maps from $\mathcal L$ onto their complete reduced spaces. Indeed, each formula maps the relative labels bijectively to the remaining clock labels within each $s$, and the matter maps are unitary. Thus each ideal reading has marginal probability $1/N$ for every physical preparation, and its conditional remaining state is
\[
\rho_X(x)=U_X(x)\rho U_X(x)^\dagger\quad (X=A,B).
\]
Within either ideal clock, conditional dynamics between slices is $U_X(x')U_X(x)^\dagger$. Between frames the transformation is $U_B(b)U_A(a)^\dagger$. Conditional B dynamics can rearrange the A reading differently in different matter sectors. Neither frame removes the other from its complete description. A chart anchor sets coordinates for the entire physical density and can be chosen independently of the event whose probability is calculated.

A noisy report $\tilde x$ is a finite clock effect $M^X_{\tilde x}=(1-e_X)|\tilde x\rangle\langle\tilde x|+e_X|\tilde x-1\rangle\langle\tilde x-1|$. It arises from classical error probabilities in an internal pointer, or a coherent detector dilation whose unused outputs are ignored. It does not change $T$. With the uniform ideal marginal the noisy conditional state of the remaining registers is the mixture
\[
(1-e_X) U_X(\tilde x)\rho U_X(\tilde x)^\dagger
+e_X U_X(\tilde x-1)\rho U_X(\tilde x-1)^\dagger .
\]
That mixture need not be reversible or related to a different noisy readout by a unitary. A comparison of two such instruments requires measurement calibration and the access actually retained.

## Physical events, measurements and records

For any kinematic effect $0\le F\le I$, the gauge-invariant lift and its physical matrix are
\[
\bar F=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}T^{-j} F T^j,\qquad
F_{\mathcal L}=J^\dagger\bar F J=J^\dagger F J.
\]
Positivity and normalization follow from the average or isometric compression. The same physical effect in the ideal $X$ chart at any registered anchor is $U_X F_{\mathcal L} U_X^\dagger$. Matched probabilities compare this operator on the transported density. An algebra of local effects and a jointly measured physical effect are distinct. In particular, the joint record effect is the average of the applicable product before averaging the individual factors.

For pointer reports $\alpha,\beta$ and record outcome $m$, use the compressed effect of $M^A_\alpha M^B_\beta |m\rangle\langle m|_R$. The outcomes make one POVM. Its $\alpha,\beta$ filter sums over both $m$. All probabilities can be obtained from its joint distribution. Conditioning records a specified subset of outcomes, with their frequency accounted for by the filter probability.

The record $R$ is explicitly written and retained within the constrained history in the benchmark. A measurement apparatus can additionally be included by taking a finite pointer with ready state $|0\rangle_O$ and assigning the joint outcome labels to its basis. For the finite fixture the isometry
\[
|\phi\rangle\longmapsto \sum_o\sqrt{\bar F_o}\,|\phi\rangle\otimes|o\rangle_O
\]
intertwines $T$ with $T\otimes I_O$. It extends on a ready-pointer subspace to a unitary block by block in the eigenspaces of $C$. Thus the model permits a fully internal record of the gauge-invariant outcome without an exterior conditional projection. This is an ideal measurement coupling on the specified algebra and is generally nonlocal in the kinematic factors. Its inclusion specifies allowed physical operations or the corresponding encoded state with an internal pointer. It supplies no autonomous local detector dynamics, physical energy cost or account of why a particular preparation occurs. The reported core dimensions omit optional apparatus registers. The registered probabilities are the same as those from the Born rule on the joint pointer and core.
