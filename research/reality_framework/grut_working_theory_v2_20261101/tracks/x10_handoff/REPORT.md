# X10 · The handoff needs bath response as well as bath energy

The finite one-axis calculation passes its movable handoff
with a coherent sterile block and selected bath directions.
Occupations alone fail. Choosing directions by the energy
discarded at the splice is sufficient to restart the full
coupled equations, but can fail when the remaining bath is
made passive. Keeping two extra directions per signed
channel leg passed all three boundaries on all seven
tested grids. The physical H55 calculation reaches one
early Fourier mode and one sterile momentum shell.

## Interacting preparation and reciprocal source

The slab begins in the negative-energy subspace of the
whole coupled Hamiltonian. A smooth scalar drive with the
vacuum bath continuously coupled generates the joint
preparation. The finite control uses a spatial first
harmonic and a homogeneous release velocity chosen to
produce a resolvable channel difference. The same unitary
mass vertex advances the fermions and gives the scalar
impulse. The volume derivative of the same Hamiltonian
includes the bath, interaction and matched vacuum terms.
The scalar gradients contribute to the energy and pressure.
The finite pressure is the volume average in an FRW metric.
[Equations](MODEL.md)

The homogeneous rotation check gives an error of
$9.02\times 10^{-16}$. The homogeneous dense and spatial ODE
comparisons converge at second order. The static coupled
vacuum and massless work controls pass. Across the six
full slab reference runs the largest normalized
energy–pressure ledger error is $1.47\times 10^{-7}$.
The reciprocal quantum work ledger closes within
$9.22\times 10^{-8}$. The longitudinal momentum error
is below $2.2\times 10^{-15}$.

## Two tests at the same boundary

The three handoff times are 1.25, 2.25 and 3.25 in the slab
units. The protocol requires immediate energy, pressure,
longitudinal momentum and scalar-work jumps below
$10^{-6}$ in units of the reference density, with the
appropriate comoving volume for momentum. Each terminal
count and the bath energy must agree within 1% of the
reference on the same grid, subject to the declared floors.
The hybrid closure also requires the direct spread from
moving the boundary to stay below 1%.

In the interacting energy basis, occupation dephasing
preserves energy to roundoff. It changes pressure by up to
$1.54\times 10^{-4}$ and changes scalar work by up to
$3.09\times 10^{-3}$ in the same density units. Splitting
off the full sterile block instead preserves initial work
while producing an energy jump as large as
$6.82\times 10^{-3}$. Bare occupation dephasing even
adds 0.01530 in energy-density units to the static
coupled vacuum while keeping its daughter count.
[Projection results](results/handoff.json)

Keep the full sterile covariance and, on each signed
bath leg, successive directions generated by the bath
Hamiltonian acting on the coupling vector. The first
direction preserves the interaction, the scalar force
and the immediate channel populations. The measured
covariance across the retained bath boundary sets the
remaining splice energy. This selects the smallest
order per leg within a share of half the jump budget.
At frozen Hamiltonian, retaining $r$ directions per leg
preserves the first $r$ time derivatives of a sterile
observable or signed channel count. The numerical
check resolves the first discarded derivative.

## Physical H55 evidence

The reconstructed 64³ H55 Gaussian scalar seed matches the
archived initial field and gradient diagnostics within
$1.4\times 10^{-6}$ relative. The `chi` velocity RMS
differs by 0.0397%. Planar averages retain 0.0511% to
0.0877% of the initial `sigma` and `chi` spectral gradient
power, depending on axis and field. The live probe takes
the first Fourier mode on one of those axes. Those
fractions characterize the representation of the seed.
[Reconstruction](results/h55_seed.json)

In the early interval $-0.02\le tH_i\le 0.10$, the
physical N1 shell with $p_\perp=M_1/2$ evolves from the
coupled adiabatic graph of the initial scalar profile.
Its spatial sterile–bath covariance norm reaches
$2.87\times 10^{-5}$ on both timesteps, versus
$1.21\times 10^{-11}$ in the homogeneous probe.
Allowing the axial mode to receive the reciprocal force
gives a ledger residual of $1.46\times 10^{-6}$ at the
finer timestep. Integrated axial work changes by 0.84%
when halving the timestep. The added bath effect on
the axial amplitude is below the timestep variation.
The mean scalar fields and metric use the prescribed
H55 history throughout this early test.
