# X9 · Does H55 lose its preparation memory?

Parent 1473db35f88bc7266c15eee28bf44f408fb90bb9.

## Full homogeneous production

Start with the inherited X5 H55 model and slow-roll input. Make a
copy of the evolving state at the nearest timestep to $t=0.16H_i^{-1}$.
The two preparations share the same scalar and metric data, energy,
pressure, and free occupations. In the alternative preparation,
reflect the Bloch component carrying scalar work at physical momenta
below 3,000, return smoothly to the original component by 6,000, and
retain the ultraviolet tail. The transition can be a mixed covariance.
Evolve both with the reciprocal X5 Hamiltonian through its archived
diagnostic time. Compare work and free occupations and energy over
the last $0.10H_i^{-1}$, with 1% as the macroscopic sensitivity target.
Check timesteps, radial grid, pressure-work conservation and Pauli
bounds. This test isolates sterile preparation sensitivity without
sterile decay.

## Continuously coupled vacuum bath

Use the X8 diagonal lepton–Higgs spectral weight above both
positive and negative frequency thresholds to define a Hermitian
quadratic representation of the vacuum Born self-energy. Calibrate
the dispersive mass and check the width at zero drive. Keep the
coupling on throughout. Regulate the conformal invariant mass of
the bath, match the real mass on the zero-drive shell, and
check the bath spacing, regulator and timestep independently.

On the prescribed fine, collisionless H55 background, use the
interval from the early copy time through $t=0.42H_i^{-1}$ and a
set of modes selected from the archived energy distribution.
Prepare two sign choices of the imaginary coherence between a
sterile-centered packet and a bath packet in the positive spectral
subspace. Match the initial sterile marginal, bath occupations,
number and energy. Compare positive sterile count and bath energy
and counts at the endpoint and over the last 0.02. Track the
interaction energy, scalar work and mass redshift. The paired
differences are a linear response of the Gaussian model. This run
uses a common prescribed scalar trace.

In a finite control with live scalar feedback, verify an exact
reciprocal energy ledger and test whether the same matched
preparations give distinguishable later scalar and bath energies.
Compare with a stationary continuum or stationary bath control
where possible. Scope the result to the quadratic vacuum model.
Full Yukawa bath interactions, physical spatial H55 initial data
and cosmological relic evolution have their own acceptance gates.
