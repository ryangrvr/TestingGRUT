# CP-11 magnetic-incidence test: a physical loop-interference mechanism, not yet spatial geometry

**2026-10-11.** Independent additive research rooted at the [frozen CP-11 commit bbbe393](https://github.com/ryangrvr/TestingGRUT/blob/bbbe393ca36a449c45ed59e02c5a385857aa1b7f/research/constructive_physics/attempt11/LAW.md). This is a **declared counterfactual extension**, NOT the CP-11 law, CP-12, Stage-3 Card 3, a revised frozen construction or a proposed merger. No parameter of CP-11 is secretly changed. The research question is whether *gauge interference generated by the same matter hopping* can overturn CP-11's rigorous zero-flux penalty for short spatial loops.

## 1. What CP-11 already establishes

In the frozen model the occupied-edge incidence matrix B_G is real and its fixed-M free-fermion ground energy, at mass m and hopping t, is
\[
U_0(G)=\kappa |E(G)|-\operatorname{Tr}\big[\sqrt{m^2 I+t^2 B_G B_G^\dagger}-mI\big].
\]
At frozen m=t=1, kappa=1/2, Gamma=1/10, degree-three locally tree-like sequences (energy per vertex approximately -0.197366056730) defeat the supplied honeycomb, square and other lattice sequences in the conditional comparison. Quantum graph flips at the frozen Gamma/N strength cannot overcome a fixed extensive energy deficit. For any fixed beta>0, the FULL original quantum graph-fermion Gibbs ensemble has limiting edge density \(1/(1+e^{\beta\kappa})>0\). **Those two failures remain recorded and are not erased by this note.**

## 2. New supplied ingredient: unitary transport on connections

Introduce a **hypothetical** U(1) gauge phase \(e^{i\phi_e}\) on each oriented connection (for executable control, restrict to Z2 phases 0 or pi). Define the *magnetic incidence*
\[
B_{ie}(\phi)=1,\quad B_{je}(\phi)=-e^{i\phi_e}
\quad\text{for oriented edge } e=(i,j).
\]
The extension must ultimately provide genuine gauge transformation rules, conjugate electric fields, a gauge Hamiltonian and a finite-energy, gauge-invariant preparation. **None is provided here**. We use this field as a conditional spectral control, not as a physical theory already selected.

The corresponding *same-coupling* fermionic energy is
\[
\boxed{U_\phi(G)=\kappa |E(G)|-\operatorname{Tr}\big[\sqrt{I+B_G(\phi)B_G(\phi)^\dagger}-I\big].}
\]
The one-body Dirac block structure and its singular-value derivation are unchanged. An important exact stability bound also survives **all** phase assignments: since Tr(B_phi B_phi^dagger)=2|E|, concavity and Jensen give U_phi/N >= kappa e - sqrt(1+2e)+1, with e=|E|/N. At the frozen kappa=1/2 this is >= -1/4 for every graph, so the magnetic-phase comparator has not purchased its loop benefit by breaking the original conditional extensive lower bound. On a forest all phases can be removed by vertex gauge transformations, so \(U_\phi(G)\) is *identical* to \(U_0(G)\) for every phase assignment. Nontrivial magnetic flux is observable only on graph cycles. This is an exact algebraic distinction between a field that affects propagation and a hand-inserted local loop reward. We are nonetheless **supplying phase degrees of freedom** and must eventually explain their dynamics.

## 3. An exact four-cycle effect

At zero flux, the four-cycle's magnetic Laplacian has eigenvalues 0,2,2,4. At pi flux its eigenvalues are \(2-\sqrt 2\), \(2-\sqrt 2\), \(2+\sqrt 2\), \(2+\sqrt 2\). For 4 vertices, 4 edges, and frozen kappa=1/2:

| Four-site graph | Conditional filled-fermion energy |
|---|---:|
| Path P4, ANY edge phases | -0.592333923934 |
| Four-cycle C4, zero flux | -0.700169592638 |
| Four-cycle C4, pi flux | **-0.720566232730** |
| Complete K4, zero flux | -0.708203932499 |
| Complete K4, best binary phase | **-0.841903733771** |

The square loop gains about 0.02039664 from a magnetic flux that is meaningful only because matter traverses an actual cycle. But a flux-optimized **complete** graph gains still more. Exhaustive testing of all 64 labelled four-vertex graph edge sets, and every binary phase assignment over present edges, finds a complete graph as the lowest *conditional* graph.

This is an adversarial check showing **the mechanism does not simply "prefer squares"**. It also shows a local loop benefit is not sufficient for physical dimensionality or sparse quantum geometry.

## 4. Thermodynamic square-lattice benchmark reverses the same-degree competition

For a supplied, infinitely extended square lattice with a pi flux per elementary square, the magnetic adjacency bands are
\[
a_\pm(k_x,k_y)=\pm2\sqrt{\cos^2 k_x+\cos^2 k_y}.
\]
The magnetic Laplacian spectrum is \(\lambda_\pm=4-a_\pm\). In the frozen CP-11 conditional functional, the resulting energy density is
\[
u_{\square,\pi}
=2-\left\langle\frac{\sqrt{5+a(k)}+\sqrt{5-a(k)}}{2}\right\rangle_{k\in[-\pi,\pi]^2},
\quad a(k)=2\sqrt{\cos^2 k_x+\cos^2 k_y}.
\]
Independent Brillouin-zone quadratures with 128, 256 and 512 nodes per axis agree to better than 1e-10. A separate Kesten–McKay integral evaluates the fixed-degree locally tree-like comparator.

| Frozen kappa,m,t conditional benchmark | Energy per vertex |
|---|---:|
| Square lattice, zero flux | -0.184539293475 |
| Four-regular locally tree-like, any phases (gauge removable locally) | -0.186535041050 |
| **Square lattice, pi flux** | **-0.188160689705** |
| Three-regular locally tree-like | **-0.197366056730** |

Thus pi flux lowers the square's conditional energy by 0.003621396230 **per vertex**, and reverses the original same-degree inequality by 0.001625648655 per vertex. This is the strongest constructive result of the probe. The reversal is not due to changing kappa, t or m, and is not an explicit plaquette-count Hamiltonian.

But the square STILL LOSES to the degree-three tree benchmark by 0.009205367025 per vertex. We have not optimized all phases on all finite-degree competing graph families and cannot claim selection of any spatial lattice over the full graph ensemble. The infinite square lattice, the flux background and its momentum coordinates are also **supplied for this comparison**, not dynamically produced.

Although magnetic adjacency can have Dirac-like band crossings, the frozen Dirac-block fermions have a nonzero m=1 gap. This result is not a massless relativistic fermion, a graviton or a Lorentzian causal geometry.

**Prior art:** Lieb, "Flux Phase of the Half-Filled Band", Phys. Rev. Lett. 73, 2158 (1994), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.2158 . Lieb's actual theorem concerns a different hopping/Hubbard system, half-filling and specific planar bipartite geometry assumptions. It supports the physics motivation for dynamical flux; it is **not a proof** of the present CP-11-extension energy comparisons or of graph selection.

## 5. Finite-temperature entropy still wins for the transparent uniform-phase extension

A clean way to extend the phase *counting* without hiding a geometric prior is one independent binary phase for every **potential** connection, whether occupied or not, with a uniform measure over all \(2^M\) phase assignments. The phase Hilbert/ensemble dimension is then the same for every graph. Suppose the phase only changes the magnetic incidence in the fermion hopping and has no additional extensive phase-dependent gauge-action term.

For each graph and phase configuration, \(\mathrm{Tr}(B_\phi B_\phi^\dagger)=2Q\), as in the real-incidence case. The fermionic hopping block has operator norm bounded by an \(O(N^{3/2})\) estimate, irrespective of phases. With CP-11's original \(O(N)\) graph-mixing operator and fixed-M fermions, write
\[
H_{\mathrm{ext}}=\kappa Q+R_N,\qquad \|R_N\|=O(N^{3/2})=o(M).
\]
The phase register gives an overall degeneracy \(2^M\) to the independent-edge reference partition function. Operator bounds and convexity therefore yield, for every fixed beta>0,
\[
\frac{\log Z_N}{M}\longrightarrow\log2+\log(1+e^{-\beta\kappa}),\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\langle Q\rangle_{\beta}}{M}\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta\kappa}}>0.}
\]
This **conditional thermal extension** still has asymptotically dense mean connectivity. It is not a proof for a genuine dynamical gauge field with a new extensive interaction, since no such field Hamiltonian is specified or tested. If extra phase states are instead assigned *only to occupied links*, the phase combinatorics changes the Bernoulli reference itself; this is an additional measure choice and likewise need not support sparsity.

At leading independent-edge counting, sparse mean edge count Q=O(N) at fixed beta requires \(p\sim c/N\) rather than a constant, equivalently a supplied effective cost
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}(N)\sim \beta^{-1}\log N+O(1),
\]
or a **physically earned restriction on accessible link configurations/measure**, or collective energy of order N² that changes the ensemble free-energy competition. A hand-set N-dependent kappa is not accepted as an earned repair; these are necessary scale indications, not a proposed law.

## 6. Decisive next *construction* rather than another selector by assertion

Two independent ingredients are still missing and cannot be disguised:

1. **Spatial organization:** A derived dynamical gauge flux can favor some loops by matter interference; the conditional square pi-flux reversal is concrete evidence the sign of CP-11's loop preference is not universal across microscopic couplings. However, the complete-graph finite counterexample and the lower degree-three tree energy block any selection claim. Need one Hamiltonian with mobile gauge fields, a physical flux cost, conservation/Gauss constraints, and an honest cross-valence + nongeometric competitor comparison.
2. **Finite-temperature state space:** With O(N²) freely active potential edges, a subquadratic-norm matter correction cannot suppress fixed-temperature dense-edge entropy. A distinct *physical* principle must account for why only O(N) connections are accessible (e.g. an actual conserved finite bond-carrier inventory, not an after-the-fact degree cap), or demonstrate an O(N²) interaction/free-energy competition, without writing a favored-dimensional graph into the premise.

**Neither is enough alone.** Even a sparse gauge-flux graph ensemble need not have polynomial volume growth, a metric light cone or Einstein dynamics. A successor must couple source/detector transport to its dynamically generated and preferentially occupied geometry and reproduce physical Lorentz-compatible propagation. The law cannot assume the intended dimension.

This is a **successful adversarial mechanism probe with an unsuccessful full selector**, not CP-12, not a changed CP-11, and not a discovery of gravity.
