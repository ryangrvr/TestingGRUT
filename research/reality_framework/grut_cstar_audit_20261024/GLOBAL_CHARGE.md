# Can the X charge survive C*?

**The current EFT conserves X number, but C* does not supply its ultraviolet
protection.** There is no quantum-gravity matching calculation here fixing a
violation coefficient. The new result is a conditional response calculation
showing which explicit violations the reference object can tolerate.

## What is established at each level

At Candidate 1's stated EFT scope, U(1)_X acts on a complex gauge-singlet
scalar. There are no X-charged chiral fermions producing the usual ABJ or
mixed gravitational chiral anomaly, and counterterms preserving the imposed
symmetry conserve its current. This is the theory actually simulated.
It does not establish nonperturbative quantum-gravity protection.

Harlow–Ooguri establish a no-global-symmetry result within specified
AdS/CFT assumptions. Applying the broad quantum-gravity expectation to
our compact cosmology is a further extrapolation. Neither the result nor
C* supplies the coefficients, scale or dominant local operators of breaking.
Finite information and closure alone also do not constitute that theorem:
an explicit finite constrained quantum toy here has an exact U(1).

Thus there are two distinct claims. As a low-energy model with a stated
symmetry, Candidate 1 remains internally defined. As a fundamental model
requiring an exact ordinary global charge in quantum gravity, it has a
missing—and under a universal no-globals principle incompatible—assumption.
The appropriate repair is protected approximate X number or a genuine gauge
completion, with its effects computed.

## Do not begin automatically at dimension five

Without the imposed X symmetry, the Standard Model gauge group and the
two scalar sign symmetries permit X tadpoles, X^2, X^3, X^4 and mixed
terms such as phi^2 X and (Hdagger H)X, plus conjugates. Curvature and
derivative operators bring additional possibilities. The scalar monomial
enumeration in CLOSURE_CHECKS.json is explicitly not the full covariant EFT.

Setting lower-dimensional violations to zero therefore needs a matching
condition or protection. The probes below assume they have already been
excluded. They do not claim that quantum gravity necessarily starts at
dimension five or chooses order-one coefficients.

## A rate calculated on the actual reference object

Probe the otherwise unchanged C1-HP-03/v1 ground profile with

\[
\delta V=\frac{c_d}{\mathcal M^{d-4}}X^d+{\rm h.c.},\qquad d=5,6.
\]

The calculation keeps Q=200000, omega/m_X=.86119513 and the same quartic
two-field response. It solves the sourced three-channel radiation problem,
including both charged signs and the real scalar, with no incoming waves.
The coefficients are hypothetical additions; the original action's c_d=0
results remain unchanged. A complete modified potential/UV theory and
its stability are not inferred from this weak perturbation.

For unit coefficient and the reduced Planck suppression scale
M=2.435e18 GeV, the initial fractional **core-charge** loss rates are
approximately 1.54e-12 /s for d=5 and 3.92e-50 /s for d=6. The d=5 emitted
anti-X is relativistic. The scalar channel also contributes; a free-particle
decay estimate is not a substitute for coherent object radiation.

Use one percent core-charge retention loss over 13.8 Gyr as an explicit
engineering benchmark, not an observational dark-matter bound. In the
frozen-profile, leading-response approximation:

- The d=5 unit-coefficient rate fails that weak-loss target. It requires
  approximately |c_5| <= 1.23e-4 at the Planck scale, or an equivalent
  suppression scale near 2e22 GeV for unit coefficient.
- The d=6 unit-coefficient Planck probe gives rate-times-age about 1.71e-32.
  Its equivalent unit-coefficient scale for this target is approximately
  8.8e10 GeV.

These are conditions on a specified operator. They are not a prediction that
Candidate 1 decays at either rate. A rate-times-age much greater than one
flags failure of the weak-loss criterion; it is not a valid evolved history
of a fixed profile after it has lost most of its charge. Other operators,
profile evolution, quantum/multiparticle channels, hot formation, deposition
and the eventual population require separate calculations.

At first order the classical charge-breaking torque oscillates and has
zero cycle average on the unperturbed rotating profile. Its bounded
excursion is not a secular lifetime. The leading outgoing radiative loss
is quadratic in |c_d|, which is why integrating the maximum first-order
torque over a Hubble time would give a false estimate.

The recorded solver/domain and background-table controls are described in
[MATHEMATICS.md](MATHEMATICS.md). No unknown coefficient is selected by
this numerical agreement.

## A concrete protection candidate, with obligations

An exact **discrete gauge** Z_N can leave accidental approximate U(1)_X
at low energies while permitting only net violations in multiples of N.
It is distinct from simply postulating another exact global symmetry.
In the enumerated analytic scalar sector, Z_5 first permits X^5 and Z_6
first permits X^6. A residual gauge Z_6 therefore passes this particular
leading-operator benchmark at a sufficiently high matching scale; Z_5 with
an order-one Planck X^5 coefficient does not.

One possible starting Lagrangian would gauge X charge 1 and introduce a
heavy Higgs Sigma of charge 6. Its condensate leaves Z_6, and

\[
\delta V=\frac{c\,\Sigma^\dagger X^6}{\mathcal M^3}+{\rm h.c.},
\qquad\langle\Sigma\rangle=v_\Sigma/\sqrt2,
\]

induces the effective X^6 term with coefficient c v_Sigma/(sqrt(2) mathcal M^3).
The vector is massive, so a sufficiently high-scale completion need not
introduce a long-range Coulomb force into the low-energy branch.

This is a proposed completion, not a completed one. The Higgs/vector
parameters, allowed portals, low-energy mass matching, compensating Higgs
charge required by compact-space Gauss constraints, corrected object
profiles and all leading decay channels must be specified and checked.
An exact residual charge modulo six alone is not an absolute stability
theorem for a large-charge object. Candidate 1's other exact global/sign
symmetries and Dirac-neutrino assumption also need their own protection.

**Decision:** C* strengthens the need for a UV story; it neither proves the
current EFT impossible nor supplies that story. The existing exact global
assumption cannot be promoted into a fundamental result. The calculated
Wilson-coefficient and scale conditions give a concrete next compatibility
test, and the residual-gauge route is a candidate to build rather than an
escape already certified.
