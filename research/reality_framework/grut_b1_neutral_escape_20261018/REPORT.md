## Physical mechanism and result

A fixed Hamiltonian converts one excited neutral fuel into a co-located
opposite-charge composite. Its constituents are confined by positive
electric energy. Second-order physical charge hopping moves the composite
away from the conversion site, reducing return during a useful finite
window. Conversion and recombination remain present throughout. Fuel is
near the composite band; no unmodeled energy sink or controller is used.

law has an explicit time-domain approximation, with all charged virtual

## What actually ran

Frozen exact physical matrices: one resource on 2, 8 and 16 events; two
resources on a common 8-event path with all 773 permitted states. No
relative-separation cutoff, discarded reverse conversion, or small-bath
replacement. The one-resource 16-event control at t=15360 gives matter
probability 0.996551 and distance>=4 co-pair arrival 0.879595. These are
finite observations, not substitutes for the continuous-time/all-size proof.
The two-event control returns below threshold, showing why a conversion
cell alone is insufficient. The two-cell control also exhibits real
collisions: an adjacent co-pair costs 1/2 more than separated pairs.

## Assumption price and boundary

New neutral conversion, near-band fuel energy, weak contact, endpoint-degree
counterterm and color symmetry are supplied. Relational interaction support,
path grammar, quantum mechanics, recording and fuel preparation also remain
inputs. The result provides a finite conditional one-composite quantum
medium; it does not establish extensive autonomous matter, a long-lived
collision regime, dimensional selection, universal locality, gravity or a
distinctive prediction about nature.

The strongest coherent answer is narrower than a populated world: spatial
escape into a mobile composite band can separate production and use in one
fixed reversible law. The bounded cycle ends here. Its single-resource
proof must be reviewed before promotion; a many-carrier theory is unproved.
