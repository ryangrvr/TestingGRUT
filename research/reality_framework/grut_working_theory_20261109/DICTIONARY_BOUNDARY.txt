Finite-dimensional boundary of the source-current dictionary
============================================================

Scope: self-contained finite-matrix derivations, with no new physical
simulation or production-data evaluation. This note constrains a current
form (a double commutator). Agreement of that form is necessary for the
adopted source response, but does not establish the full observable map,
all detector probabilities, a continuum theorem, or empirical GRUT.

1. An exact obstruction to an all-state finite-dimensional dictionary
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Let H and G be Hermitian matrices on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of
dimension d>0, and define

  S(G) = [G,[H,G]] = 2GHG - G^2 H - H G^2.

Cyclicity of the finite matrix trace gives Tr S(G)=0. S(G) is Hermitian,
so its smallest eigenvalue is <=0. For every c>0,

  ||S(G)-c I||_op >= c.

Proof: on an eigenvector of S(G) with eigenvalue s_min<=0, the difference
has absolute eigenvalue |s_min-c|>=c. Equivalently the maximally mixed state
rho_*=I/d has Tr(rho_* S(G))=0, while the proposed positive continuum
c-number current has expectation c. There is no finite-dimensional model
with relative error strictly below 100% uniformly over every state for
this positive c-number identity. This is independent of numerical accuracy.

For commuting Hermitian sources G_i, Jacobi's identity gives a symmetric
Hermitian source-current matrix of operators

  S_ij=[G_i,[H,G_j]],  S_ij=S_ji.

Let C>0 be the target continuum current matrix and let S(rho)_ij=Tr rho S_ij.
For rho_*, S(rho_*)=0, hence

  || C^(-1/2) [S(rho_*)-C] C^(-1/2) ||_op = 1.

The worst-state normalized discrepancy is therefore at least one. A pure
state witness also exists for each nonzero source direction by applying
the scalar proof to G(u)=sum_i u_i G_i. Commutativity is used for the
matrix-current symmetry; the scalar trace obstruction needs only H,G
Hermitian.

This does NOT exclude convergence on a physically specified set of states,
a low-energy sector, selected matrix elements, or finite-resolution
experiments. It excludes an unqualified uniform all-state identification
at fixed finite Hilbert-space dimension. A continuum limit must explicitly
specify its state domain, source test functions, topology of convergence,
and error control. The trace argument by itself proves no such limit.

2. The correct state-restricted current statement
------------------------------------------------
Fix the physical state family R BEFORE testing, including every actually
reached conditional state for the proposed experiment. The operational
current requirement is

  sup_{rho in R} || C^(-1/2)[S(rho)-C]C^(-1/2) ||_op <= epsilon.

A finite collection of tested states proves only those comparisons unless
a separate argument covers the rest of R. Convex mixtures of a certified
collection inherit the same bound by convexity. Postselection generally
does not stay in that convex hull and is not covered automatically.

If R is every density matrix supported on a specified orthogonal projector
P, put G_u=sum_i (C^(-1/2)u)_i G_i for real unit source vectors u. Then the
exact equivalent criterion is

  sup_{||u||=1} || P([G_u,[H,G_u]]-I)P ||_op <= epsilon,

where the norm is on the range of P. This follows by writing the symmetric
source-matrix norm as sup_u |u^T(.)u| and then optimizing the scalar
expectation over all states supported on P. The compressed current can
be positive even though its full-space trace is zero: compression changes
the state domain. A source-by-Hilbert-space block-operator bound is a
sufficient stronger certificate, but should not be silently presented as
equivalent; it can also test source/state-entangled vectors that the
operational criterion never uses.

Compression must keep the full intermediate transitions. In general
P[G,[H,G]]P is NOT [PGP,[PHP,PGP]] evaluated as an operator on range(P).
Replacing H and G by their compressed matrices before forming the double
commutator can discard transitions through the complementary space and
reintroduces the finite-trace obstruction in an inappropriate truncated
model. The two-dimensional toy accompanying this note explicitly checks
this distinction.

3. Exact kick law, including initial currents
--------------------------------------------
On bond j write H_j=t_j(T_j+T_j^dagger), J_j=i t_j(T_j-T_j^dagger), and fix
the orientation by [F,T_j]=w_j T_j for the real detector weights w_j.
For U_kappa=exp(i kappa F), direct conjugation gives

  U_kappa^dagger H_j U_kappa
    = cos(kappa w_j) H_j - sin(kappa w_j) J_j.

Let h_j=<H_j>, q_j=<J_j> in the initial state and let v_j be the real column
of primary source weights. For B_j=v_j v_j^T>=0, the frozen lattice current
is

  S_0=-sum_j h_j B_j,
  S_kappa=sum_j [-h_j cos(kappa w_j)+q_j sin(kappa w_j)] B_j.

Thus an initial imaginary bond current contributes a sine term. Its sign
depends on the declared hopping orientation; all bounds below use absolute
values and are independent of that convention. Vanishing q_j is an
additional state property, not a consequence of the numerical norm of h_j.

4. PSD special case and its robust extension
-------------------------------------------
If every h_j<=0 and every q_j=0, define A_j=-h_j B_j>=0. Then

  D_kappa=S_0-S_kappa=sum_j [1-cos(kappa w_j)]A_j,
  0<=D_kappa<=delta S_0,
  delta=max_j[1-cos(kappa w_j)]<=min(2,kappa^2 w_max^2/2).

For S_0>0 and C>0, define

  d_kappa=||S_0^(-1/2)(S_kappa-S_0)S_0^(-1/2)||_op,
  e_kappa=||C^(-1/2)(S_kappa-C)C^(-1/2)||_op,
  e_0=||C^(-1/2)(S_0-C)C^(-1/2)||_op.

Then d_kappa<=delta and e_kappa<=e_0+(1+e_0)delta. These inequalities do not
require positive cosines. They follow from PSD order and the triangle
inequality, not a truncated Taylor expansion.

For positive h_j or nonzero q_j, define the PSD defect matrices

  A_-=sum_j max(h_j,0) B_j,
  Q_abs=sum_j |q_j| B_j,
  eta_M=||M^(-1/2) A_- M^(-1/2)||_op,
  j_M=||M^(-1/2) Q_abs M^(-1/2)||_op,
  sigma=max_j |sin(kappa w_j)|<=min(1,|kappa|w_max).

Writing A_+=sum_j max(-h_j,0)B_j gives S_0=A_+-A_- and
A_++A_-=S_0+2A_-. Each coefficient of D_kappa lies between its negative
and positive absolute bound, so for any positive metric M,

  ||M^(-1/2)D_kappa M^(-1/2)||_op
    <= delta ||M^(-1/2)(A_++A_-)M^(-1/2)||_op + sigma j_M.

Consequently

  d_kappa <= delta(1+2 eta_S0) + sigma j_S0,
  e_kappa <= e_0 + delta(1+e_0+2 eta_C) + sigma j_C.

These bounds remain valid when some initial hoppings are positive. They
make explicit the additional budget required to allow such bonds and
initial currents. The q term is generally linear in |kappa| near zero;
one cannot retain the purely quadratic kick budget unless that term is
certified absent or small.

5. Incorporating certified input uncertainty
-------------------------------------------
Suppose certified intervals, not merely central floating-point estimates,
give |h_j-hhat_j|<=r_hj, |q_j-qhat_j|<=r_qj, and |w_j|<=wbar_j. With exact
source vectors v_j and exact positive target C, form

  Shat_0=-sum_j hhat_j B_j,
  E_h=sum_j r_hj B_j,
  eta_h=||C^(-1/2)E_h C^(-1/2)||_op,
  ebar_0=||C^(-1/2)(Shat_0-C)C^(-1/2)||_op+eta_h,
  Abar_-=sum_j max(hhat_j+r_hj,0) B_j,
  Qbar_abs=sum_j (|qhat_j|+r_qj) B_j.

Then e_0<=ebar_0, A_-<=Abar_- and Q_abs<=Qbar_abs. If ebar_0<1, it also
certifies S_0>0. Define eta_bar and j_bar by whitening Abar_- and Qbar_abs
with C. Since S_0>=(1-ebar_0)C, the robust simultaneous sufficient bounds
are

  d_kappa <= delta[1+2 eta_bar/(1-ebar_0)]
                       +sigma j_bar/(1-ebar_0),
  e_kappa <= ebar_0+delta(1+ebar_0+2 eta_bar)+sigma j_bar,

where delta<=kappa^2 wbar_max^2/2 and sigma<=|kappa|wbar_max may be used
globally, with optional caps 2 and 1 respectively. No sign certification
is needed for this robust version; certifying hhat_j+r_hj<=0 removes the
positive-hopping term. Certifying |qhat_j|+r_qj=0 removes the sine term.

If source vectors are also calibrated with ||v_j-vhat_j||<=r_vj, work in a
fixed metric C: qhatv_j=C^(-1/2)vhat_j and r_zj=||C^(-1/2)||r_vj. Then

  ||zz^T-qhatv_j qhatv_j^T|| <= 2||qhatv_j||r_zj+r_zj^2.

A conservative contribution to the baseline current error is

  sum_j { |hhat_j|[2||qhatv_j||r_zj+r_zj^2]
           +r_hj(||qhatv_j||+r_zj)^2 }.

Similarly eta_bar and j_bar can be upper-bounded by replacing each
whitened rank-one norm with (||qhatv_j||+r_zj)^2 and summing the positive
coefficient bounds. These are conservative scalar bounds, not tight fits.

If the target current C_true itself has certified relative uncertainty
||Cbar^(-1/2)(C_true-Cbar)Cbar^(-1/2)||<=eta_C<1, a certified error ebar
relative to Cbar implies

  ||C_true^(-1/2)(S-C_true)C_true^(-1/2)||
    <= (ebar+eta_C)/(1-eta_C).

Use this conversion in the final physical allowance; do not absorb target
calibration error into a fitted normalization. Independence of calibration
data and held-out observations is an experimental obligation outside the
finite-matrix proof.

6. A closed sufficient radius with the uncertainty terms retained
----------------------------------------------------------------
For exact target C and ebar_0<1, let tau be the fixed allowance and epsilon
any additional reserve not already counted in the certified uncertainties.
Require tau>epsilon+ebar_0. Define

  a_d=(wbar_max^2/2)[1+2 eta_bar/(1-ebar_0)],
  b_d=wbar_max j_bar/(1-ebar_0),
  a_e=(wbar_max^2/2)(1+ebar_0+2 eta_bar),
  b_e=wbar_max j_bar,
  m_d=tau-epsilon,  m_e=tau-epsilon-ebar_0.

The two inequalities a_d r^2+b_d r<=m_d and a_e r^2+b_e r<=m_e certify
both current requirements for every |kappa|<=r. For a>0, b>=0, m>0, the
positive root can be evaluated stably as

  R(a,b,m)=2m/[b+sqrt(b^2+4am)].

Take r=min(R(a_d,b_d,m_d),R(a_e,b_e,m_e)). When a=0,b>0 use m/b; when
a=b=0 the corresponding inequality imposes no finite radius. Negative
m means this sufficient rule provides no interval around zero. This
derivation is predictive only after all input bounds, state domain,
allowances, and reserve allocations are fixed.

7. Why a small ground-state residual does not certify these inputs
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let psi be normalized, Ehat=<psi,H psi>, r=||(H-Ehat)psi||. Even r=0 does
not identify the ground state: an exact excited eigenvector is a direct
counterexample. A gap value without an energy/branch identification does
not fix that problem.

For a unique ground state Omega, suppose an independent spectral
certificate gives E_1>=L_1>Ehat, with L_1 a valid lower bound on every
excited energy. Put d=L_1-Ehat>0. Expanding psi in exact eigenvectors,

  r^2=sum_n |c_n|^2(E_n-Ehat)^2
      >=d^2 sum_{n>=1}|c_n|^2.

Therefore the excited norm is <=r/d and

  || |psi><psi|-|Omega><Omega| ||_1 <=2 min(1,r/d).

For any bounded Hermitian observable A this implies

  |<A>_psi-<A>_Omega| <=2||A|| min(1,r/d),

or the tighter spectral-range(A)*min(1,r/d) after centering A. Apply such
bounds to each H_j and J_j to obtain the input intervals above. A certified
ground-energy enclosure and excited-energy lower bound can supply the
needed branch isolation. In a degenerate ground space, this certifies
distance to that subspace, not a unique physical ground-state density
matrix or its detector predictions.

Hamiltonian calibration must also enter the spectral/state certificate.
For H=Hhat+DeltaH, ||DeltaH||<=epsilon_H, and a directional source
G=Ghat+DeltaG, ||DeltaG||<=epsilon_G, direct commutator norm inequalities
give

  ||[G,[H,G]]-[Ghat,[Hhat,Ghat]]||
  <=4 epsilon_H(||Ghat||+epsilon_G)^2
      +8||Hhat||||Ghat||epsilon_G+4||Hhat||epsilon_G^2.

Scalar identity offsets can be removed before taking these norms because
they do not affect commutators. A separate FULL trace-norm state error
epsilon_rho=||rho-rhohat||_1 contributes at most
epsilon_rho ||[Ghat,[Hhat,Ghat]]|| to the
directional expectation error. The resulting directional error bounds
must be uniform over the normalized source directions to certify the
matrix criterion. An arbitrary reserve is not a substitute for these
certified calibration and state-error bounds.

8. What remains uncertified in the archived N22 result
----------------------------------------------------
The earlier |kappa|<=0.7057 result was a guarded algebraic consequence of
the archived N22/N22_refined central matrices. It was not based on a
rigorous ground-gap/branch certificate, interval arithmetic for those
inputs, or a physical calibration error budget. This note does not
retroactively upgrade it. The registered numerical comparisons and their
failures remain unchanged. A certified continuum dictionary needs its
specified state domain and continuum error proof in addition to this
finite current-form bound. A calibrated physical realization needs
independent parameter/state calibration, a frozen prediction with a
complete error budget, and held-out experimental data. Current-form
agreement alone supplies neither milestone.

9. A finite-duration controlled source pulse has an explicit error budget
------------------------------------------------------------------------
For the finite chain's directed nearest-neighbor hop T_j, T_j^2=0 and
T_j+T_j^dagger and T_j-T_j^dagger have operator norm at most one (one on
an allowed two-state hopping block). With H_j=(T_j+T_j^dagger)/(2a),
all other terms diagonal and commuting with F, and [F,T_j]=w_j T_j,

  ||[H,F]|| <= sum_j |w_j|/(2a) =: B_F.

This bound is valid also after restriction to a charge sector invariant
under H and F. Additional hardware Hamiltonian terms must be separately
bounded; they cannot be silently assumed to commute with the source.

Center the pulse time interval on zero, t in [-tau/2,tau/2], and use
H(t)=H-u(t)F with signed area kappa=int u(t)dt. In the interaction frame
of H, F_I(t)=exp(iHt)F exp(-iHt), and integration of its derivative gives
||F_I(t)-F||<=|t| B_F. The unitary Duhamel identity therefore yields

  ||U_I-exp(i kappa F)|| <= B_F int |u(t)| |t|dt.

For a centered flat pulse u(t)=kappa/tau, this is

  delta_U <= |kappa| tau B_F/4.

The factor 1/4 is the exact centered flat-profile first absolute time
moment, not a series estimate. A pulse profile, area, or timing uncertainty
requires a certified upper bound on the corresponding integral. For a
flat pulse centered at a timing error t0, the simple bound
int |u||t|dt <= |kappa|(tau/4+|t0|) is sufficient.

The comparison is in the interaction frame. In the laboratory frame the
instantaneous ideal comparator is

  exp(-iH tau/2) exp(i kappa F) exp(-iH tau/2),

which retains the free half-intervals. Comparing directly with exp(i kappa
F) while ignoring free evolution would give a false zero error at kappa=0.
For a controlled source unitary the same operator bound holds on the
ancilla-controlled block. Affine offsets of F remain physical controlled
phase calibrations; their commutator vanishes but they cannot be omitted
from a prediction for interferometric detector probabilities.

For the lattice current of a source direction u, write z_j=v_j^T u. Its
operator is S_u=-sum_j z_j^2 H_j. Consequently

  ||S_u|| <= sum_j z_j^2/(2a)
           = (pi/2) u^T C u,
  C=(1/(pi a)) sum_j v_j v_j^T.

If D_tr(rho,sigma)=(1/2)||rho-sigma||_1, the expectation inequality gives

  ||C^(-1/2)[S(rho)-S(sigma)]C^(-1/2)|| <= pi D_tr(rho,sigma).

For unitary operators U,V on the same input state,
D_tr(U rho U^dagger,V rho V^dagger)<=||U-V||. Therefore a finite-pulse
implementation of the ideal instantaneous kick adds at most pi delta_U
to its current discrepancy; a separately bounded preparation error
D_prep adds at most pi D_prep. Use min(1,D_prep+delta_U) if desired. These
terms are added to the ideal-kick budget without double-counting state or
operator errors already included in the coefficient intervals. They do
not automatically cover selected detector branches, whose normalization
requires a separately certified probability floor and error propagation.

The archived 0.7057 radius includes neither this finite-pulse term nor a
certified exact-ground/preparation uncertainty. A physical current-only
operating condition must instead satisfy, for example,

  certified_ideal_current_error
    +pi(delta_U+D_prep)+certified_operator_error+other_reserved_errors
    <= fixed_allowance,

with each term justified and fixed before evaluating held-out data. This
is a bounded finite-duration condition; a physically zero-width pulse is
not assumed. Coupling-area, source-shape, Hamiltonian and detector phase
calibration uncertainties must likewise appear in the prediction budget.

10. What a full observable/instrument dictionary would additionally need
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The current form controls only one aspect of response. A sufficient route
to a full finite experiment comparison would separately specify a bounded
energy/source/reached-state domain and a validated isometry relating the
lattice and continuum state spaces on that domain. One then needs explicit
Hamiltonian and source intertwining bounds, controlled propagation leakage
outside the domain, detector-operation/channel approximation bounds,
finite-pulse and calibration errors, and normalization floors for any
conditional claims. Those bounds must be propagated through the actual
finite sequence of preparation, evolution and measurement. This note
does not supply that isometry, continuum leakage theorem, or full error
budget. No current-only result should be represented as having supplied
them. These are the precise outstanding mathematical obligations beside
the independent calibrated, held-out experimental milestone.
