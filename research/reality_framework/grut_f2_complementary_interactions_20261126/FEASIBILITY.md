# Analytical feasibility and conventional baseline

The bounded mathematical question is the **worst-case order of
independently calibrated static diagonal interventions** needed to
identify a positive spring graph through one driving-point readout.
The nearest structural-dynamics literature uses known mass or
stiffness modifications to infer unmeasured frequency responses.
The source review identified these operations and their matrix
identities. It did not establish the specific sharp comparison
between all singleton settings and finite pair settings under
positive-spring and common-pin constraints. That comparison is
suitable for exact proof. It could clarify the information supplied
by the complementary interaction even if there is no new law.
[Silva, Maia and Ribeiro](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsvi.1999.2993)
· [Guevara Vasquez et al., matrix inverse problems](https://doi.org/10.3934/nhm.2020001)
· [del Rio and Kudryavtsev, Jacobi case](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/28/5/055007)

At $z>0$ the inverse of a connected positive-spring stiffness
shift is a strictly positive real matrix. A rank-one
perturbation's exact diagonal response gives a rational function
of only the root-to-tag entry squared and the tag diagonal.
A simultaneous two-tag perturbation exposes a cross entry.
Schur complement positivity bounds this entry and selects a
unique branch of the inverse, including with dark normal modes.
A rooted pair of regular cospectral graphs with equal diagonal
spectral measures is a hostile singleton-data control. This
strategy follows standard structural modification rather than
a claim of a new microscopic interaction.
[Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury identities, Hager](https://doi.org/10.1137/1031049)

The continuum baseline is prescribed-metric Klein–Gordon theory.
The scalar conformal law trades scale for a zeroth-order
potential after conjugation by the conformal weight. Boundary
inverse problems already have associated gauge freedoms.
Conforming probe data with extra nonlinear response, well
controlled reach and more information can give positive
reconstruction theorems. Counterexamples to linear partial
boundary recovery also exist within metrics conformal to
Minkowski. Their hypotheses differ from the present
small-amplitude causal assay.

For the chosen finite time support, the flat positive light-cone
term can give an explicit lower bound on the waveform through
the bump. The massive tail and the repeated scattering on the
bump have absolute retarded bounds. The massless curvature
channel uses an integration-by-parts sign for the linearized
term, then tests a conservative nonlinear remainder.
Weak fields and their null cones alone do not supply an
inverse theorem on the space of possible backgrounds.

The feasibility gate supports exact analytical and rational work
on these questions. It gives no rationale for an expensive
dynamical search or an additional GRUT organizing or gravity
principle. Priority for a broad new theorem would require a
further independent literature and proof review.
