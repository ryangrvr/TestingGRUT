# Same-state observables and their operational boundary

This stability packet does not replace the Candidate 1 action or retune its reporter. It keeps the already calculated preparation and records a direct scattering quantity that does not require the ideal arm interferometer used in the original example.

## Elastic partial-wave observable

For a classical static target and a weak linear reporter at asymptotic momentum `p=k g_X v`, the s-wave radial solution obeys

\[
u''+[k^2-.01(S_*^2-1)]u=0,\qquad u(0)=0,
\quad u\sim\sin(k\rho+\delta_0).
\]

The elastic channel is `S_0=exp(2i delta_0)`. The usual partial-wave amplitude definition `f_l=(S_l−1)/(2ik)` and angular orthogonality give the s-wave contribution

\[
\boxed{\sigma_0(g_Xv)^2=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\sin^2\delta_0.}
\]

This is a partial contribution, not the full plane-wave total cross section. Higher partial waves were not calculated in the original benchmark, and their interference matters for differential scattering.

The phase values are inherited from the original executed reporter calculation, which used two independent ODE forms and tolerance refinements on the same solved scalar profile. The new values above are algebraic consequences of those phase outputs; no new scattering trajectory was executed here. Their printed numerical precision does not include uncomputed quantum, target-recoil, inelastic, loop or apparatus corrections.

An empirical partial-wave test would require an independently calibrated reporter mass/momentum and flux; a target with measured state/charge; sufficient angular or mode information to identify the partial wave; and a physical detector with characterized efficiency. The source and detector must use degrees of freedom and couplings allowed by the action, with their energy and losses counted. This package supplies no such microscopic apparatus. The mathematical cross section is a conditional EFT observable, not a completed experimental probability or an observed particle signal.

## Gravitational observables of that same preparation

The original weak-field calculation uses the full stationary scalar energy and anisotropic stresses, not a density-only replacement. Its central clock redshift is about `6.68813×10^−10`, and its exterior light deflection at `b g_X v=20` is `2.89601×10^−10` radians. These follow from adopted Einstein gravity with the same scalar stress and illustrative `v/M_P=10^−5`.

The nonlinear trajectories in the new packet omit the metric and reporter at leading order. They do not establish time-dependent Einstein consistency or new clock/lensing results for the deformed objects. The previously calculated small metric supports a weak-gravity hierarchy; no nonlinear gravitational response coefficient or quantum lifetime is inferred from it.

## Empirical status

All new-sector constants are illustrative inputs. No independent physical calibration, portal-running phenomenology, abundance history, experimental constraint survey or held-out observation has been completed. The zero matched portals are boundary conditions, not protected all-scale decoupling. Those obligations remain necessary before calling the benchmark a realistic model of observed matter.

Large mean/classical charge and small loop-counting factors suggest a semiclassical regime; they do not supply a renormalized fluctuation calculation, control a nonperturbative decay or validate the assumed new particles. The concrete advance here is a better tested classical preparation with explicit conditional observables.
