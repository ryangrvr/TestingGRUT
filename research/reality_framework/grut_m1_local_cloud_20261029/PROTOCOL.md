Keep the M1 action, 10% radial pulse, yield and reference core density fixed.
The last packet demonstrates an early homogeneous-gas assumption, not its
validity. This packet starts with the retained fine radial trace and makes a
time-resolved surface source. Quantify the time-frequency, positive/negative
flux, physical frequency support and outgoing/incoming ambiguities; compare
window and matching-radius choices when practical. Source spectra are
semiclassical diagnostics, not automatically occupation probabilities.

First solve a deliberately bounded test: direct outward primary X and Xbar
emitted from a spherical surface, propagated collisionlessly on radial rays,
with the resonant X Xbar <-> h rate evaluated along their worldlines. Use
unmodified widths and energy-momentum kinematics. Derive the collinear
narrow-width integral, distinguish integrated event exposure from physical
loss, and compare analytic integration with a finite-width independent
control. Evaluate overlap in actual source time and momentum and vary time
and energy resolutions. Positivity, spectral support, charge, energy and
spatial flux ledger are explicit. Test against synthetic ballistic cases with
known answers.

This direct-primary calculation excludes h decay daughters, inward-going
particles, local core scattering, non-collinear angular spread, core
excitation and gas backreaction. It cannot certify the entire local cloud or
the ultimate 1% criterion, regardless of the result. If a required matching
step is unresolved, preserve it and show the sensitivity instead of
asserting that classical Fourier intensities are unique quantum particles.

## Subsequent, explicitly bounded extensions

Track direct h-decay daughters in straight-line vacuum kinematics; estimate
their geometric return and their first-order exposure to the direct radial
primary beams, with decay timing and a finite observation horizon. Separately
track first passage to half and one density length. Finally, use identical
synchronous sources on independent Poisson centers to estimate pointwise
density fluctuations and the distinct-center mean s-channel term. These are
additional assumed models, not reconstruction of the actual core population
or proof of spatial mixing. Retain the unstable midpoint-energy attempt,
slow-tail and time-window sensitivities, and off-shell controls.
