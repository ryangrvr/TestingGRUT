## Declared matter-coupling class C

The new species b has a vacuum and a one-particle event sector, as does the
original neutral probe a. These are separately prepared experiments. The
original a species is vacuum in the b experiment. Added b terms vanish in
its vacuum, so the formation law and preparation are unchanged.

Class C requires: self-adjoint real hopping; conservation of b number and
the existing storage/receiver charge; covariance under simultaneous event
relabeling, including root and disorder data; a bounded number of event
registers per primitive summand; no coordinates or fixed event adjacency;
and a size-uniform norm on the declared vacuum/one-particle sector.
The existing distinguished root may control a term. A root-blind subclass
will also be tested. On-event energies and graph-dependent hopping may
coexist with graph-blind hopping. No bond-projector selection rule is
assumed. These are an explicit extension class, not all possible physical
matter laws or the originally frozen CP-12 charged species.

Candidates to analyze before any numerical results: a root-blind complete
graph with normalized coupling; a root-conditioned star with normalized
coupling; and that star plus a nonzero bounded occupied-bond hopping term.
All reverse transitions remain. Counterexamples must not modify geometry
formation or exploit an unbounded single-particle interaction norm.

## Deliverables and stopping rule

1. A line-by-line rederivation or a counterexample to each load-bearing S9
   estimate, with any affected claim explicitly withdrawn or narrowed.
2. A concrete distinct-sector Hamiltonian, symmetry/constraint checks and
   a proved transport consequence relative to a metric fixed by recording.
3. A concise supplied/derived/changes table, a receiver-dependence lemma
   with its hypotheses, and a primary-source literature comparison.
4. Small exact-algebra / finite-matrix controls, source hashes and a single
   reproduction command. Numerical controls are diagnostics, not all-N proof.
5. A short reviewer handoff identifying the remaining independent-review
   obligations and the next physical decision.

Stop when the bounded coupling question is answered by a robustness theorem,
counterexample, or a precise additional-assumption list. Do not respond to
a negative result by silently restricting C or opening another solver branch.
