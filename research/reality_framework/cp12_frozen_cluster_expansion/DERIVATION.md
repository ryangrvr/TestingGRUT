# Frozen-coupling edge-cluster calculation

This is a conditional ground-state energy calculation for graph-compressed
CP12. It retains Gauss constraints, fermion signs, compact integer electric
fields and the true graph-rewiring diagonal. Off-diagonal graph mixing is not
included. No hopping expansion is used for the finite cluster eigenvalues.

## Host Hamiltonian and inherited compression coefficient

For a host graph G with Q present edges, compression of the frozen law gives

    P_G H12 P_G = kappa Q + 2m N_vertex + (epsilon_E/2) sum E_leg^2
                 + t H_hop + a_N sum R_e,
    a_N = Gamma (M-Q)/M, M=N(N-1)/2.

In the Q=2N thermodynamic sector a_N -> Gamma=.1. We take this same inherited
limit a=.1 in every cluster. We do NOT recompute a from the size of the open
cluster; it is a local term inherited from its large host. The other frozen
coefficients are m=t=1,kappa=.5,epsilon_E=.25. These calculations do not rank
finite-N=729 blocks with a_N=181/1820.

For a finite edge subgraph c, use the frozen physical basis for that subgraph:
its absent edges are unexcited; each connected component has zero net Gauss
charge. The energy is additive over disconnected components. Isolated vertices
have no nontrivial physical states and contribute zero. We subtract the
extensive neutral baseline (.5+.1)|E(c)| when tabulating relative energies.
It cancels in every geometry comparison at common bond count.

## Inclusion-exclusion

For a connected graph c let E(c) be its finite-cluster ground energy. Define

    w(c)=E(c)-sum_(connected proper edge subclusters s) n(s,c) w(s).

Subgraphs here are edge subsets, with isolated vertices dropped. Embeddings
are non-induced: a host can have additional edges between mapped vertices.
An equivalent identity is

    w(c)=sum_(F subset E(c)) (-1)^(|E(c)|-|F|) E(F),

where E(F) is the sum of its connected-component ground energies. This follows
from Boolean-lattice Möbius inversion. Additivity makes disconnected weights
vanish. Consequently for every FINITE host there is an exact identity

    E(G)=sum_(connected c) n(c,G) w(c),

if every edge subset is retained. Replacing this by a thermodynamic sum of
embedding densities requires convergence; truncating by edge number is not an
energy upper or lower bound.

`clusters.py` enumerates connected bipartite clusters of maximum degree four.
The graph atlas supplies the exhaustive catalogue through six edges. Further
orders are generated by adding a leaf or a missing edge and rejecting
isomorphic duplicates. Completeness follows by removing a leaf from a tree,
or a nonbridge edge from a graph with cycles. Each reduced graph remains
connected, bipartite and of bounded degree. Hashes only group candidates;
exact graph isomorphism decides equivalence.

On a vertex-transitive host, the embedding density is the number of injective
edge-preserving maps fixing one chosen cluster vertex at a host root, divided
by the cluster automorphism count. Vertex images are distinct. This convention
is important for winding: quotienting a plane lattice into a tube both adds
closed-cycle embeddings and removes self-intersecting open-path embeddings.

The locally tree-like comparison uses the radius-eight ball of the infinite
four-regular tree. It supplies correct finite-cluster embedding densities at
these orders. It is the local limit of a random four-regular graph ensemble,
not a constructed finite four-regular tree or a computed finite random sample.

## First width-nine-sensitive contribution

Square and width-nine tube embeddings agree through eight edges. At nine
edges exactly two connected cluster topologies differ:

    c=C9: Delta m(c)=+1/9,
    c=P10 (nine-edge open path): Delta m(c)=-1.

The extra ring winds around the narrow dimension. The two oriented straight
nine-step paths that would wrap onto their starting vertex cease to be
injective; dividing their missing rooted maps by path reversal gives -1.
`embedding_boundary.py` exhaustively checks the relevant catalogue. The tube
has no odd cycle shorter than nine, so C9 is the only non-bipartite cluster
with at most nine edges that can embed. All other relevant topologies are
bipartite and generated exhaustively.

For P_n, n vertices and n-1 bonds,

    w(P_n)=E(P_n)-2E(P_(n-1))+E(P_(n-2)), n>=3.

Every proper connected edge subset of C9 is a path; there are nine embeddings
of each path length. Telescoping gives

    w(C9)=E(C9)-9[E(P9)-E(P8)].

Thus the COMPLETE first width-sensitive cluster increment is

    Delta e_(9) = w(C9)/9-w(P10)
                = E(C9)/9-[E(P10)-E(P9)].

Using only w(C9)/9 would overstate this increment by nearly an order of
magnitude in the computed frozen-coupling example. Both the initial
cycle-only diagnostic and its correction are preserved in the output.
This edge order nine has no relation to truncating the hopping series at
order nine: each finite-cluster eigenvalue includes all hopping orders at t=1.

## The first correction, edge order ten

At ten edges the unequal embedding densities belong to four branched trees,
one open path and a nine-cycle with a pendant edge. Their list and embedding
differences are in tenth_boundary_results.json. Boolean Möbius inversion
reduces their total contribution to an exact linear combination of thirteen
finite-cluster ground energies, in tenth_energy_expression.json.
The coefficient-weighted edge counts sum to zero, confirming cancellation of
the neutral baseline. These are numerical finite-cluster energies; the
expression's combinatorial coefficients are exact integers.

`tenth_energy.py` evaluates that expression without substituting an isolated
flux vertex, a graph Laplacian spectrum, a classical optimized phase, or a
weak-hopping polynomial. Its result is still an edge-truncated difference,
not a full thermodynamic energy ordering.

## Error scope

The finite physical bases retain |E_leg|<=L as a regulator. The projected rotor
raising operators are not unitary at the cutoff boundary; this is why boundary
weights and multiple cutoffs are recorded. Hamiltonian
construction asserts Gauss's law and Hermiticity. Lowest Ritz energies,
residuals and cutoff-boundary probabilities are recorded. Cross-cutoff
comparison and different eigensolver initial vectors are separate empirical
checks. No infinite-cutoff error certificate or full excited-spectrum
certificate is claimed; a symmetric starting vector can miss excitation
sectors even when its ground energy agrees with a random-start calculation.

The absence of a thermodynamic remainder bound is more consequential than
small solver residuals here. Tight finite-cluster numerical agreement does not
prove that the omitted clusters preserve the sign of a 1e-9 density increment.
