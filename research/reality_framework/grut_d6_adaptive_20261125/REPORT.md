# GRUT-D6 · Acquired self-representation and adaptive development

25 November 2026. A closed quantum system uses one internal phase
assay to estimate a previously unknown coupling sign. It stores a
binary record, leaves the source site, and uses the same record
to construct a uniform bank and a challenge-dependent alternating
bank. An independently evolving spin supplies a physical forecast
test. The weak probe gives 75% probability of both target words
and a correct forecast. Scrambling or disabling the usable record
reduces that score to 50%. The intact decoder reaches the exact
single-probe discrimination limit.

## What the model acquires

The assay applies one of two phase rotations, depending on a
conserved internal source. It starts with the same probe and
blank memory under both conditions. For assay angle θ the two
possible probe states have overlap cos(2θ). Their equal-prior
distinguishability gives optimal decision probability
(1+sin(2θ))/2 for 0≤θ≤π/4. The local Y decoder attains that
value and stores the result in M. The head leaves the used probe
at the source and carries only M as a condition-dependent input
into the primary response. The two layouts use the same decision,
so their joint success is 0.75 in the weak case, with shared
errors. A second-stage success is an additional use of the record.

The witness starts independently of the condition and receives
no signal from the writer. At T its Y outcome reveals the
coupling sign under the two open strengths. This gives an
operational forecast in addition to the prescribed tape score.
The experiment assumes that the two physical interactions share
the sign. It tests acquisition within a calibrated family.

## Causal and negative controls

In the weak case the main structural score is 0.75 intact, 0.50
scrambled, 0.25 inverted and 0.50 disabled. The passive writer
keeps the acquired record and reproduces the same distribution of
visible layouts over an even source prior, yet scores 0.50. The
extra direct source port scores 1 through all memory interventions,
showing that a bypass can produce excellent scores with no causal
memory contribution. All arms share the same one-head Hilbert
space and exact energy law at a given size.

In the sealed control, the source factorizes out of the accessible
Hamiltonian. Optimal structural success is 1/2 for any accessible
probe, including the direct-port setting. The witness becomes
constant, so a disabled writer forecasts W with probability 1
while learning zero about the source. Neither a patterned display
nor a high forecast score on such a constant benchmark establishes
a useful internal representation.

## Clock and finite system

The terminal transfer is exact at T. Throughout
$|\omega t-\pi|\le0.2$, the probability that the two banks are
unfinished is bounded by $1.049\times10^{-16}$ at n=2 and
decreases with n. This includes all clock amplitudes. The weak
joint structural and physical forecast score is at least 0.747
over the window. The sampled minimum is 0.748126561979. At the
separate A-only diagnostic the clock is in the intended stage
with probability 0.660416206084 for n=2 and 0.999867119390 for
n=64. The n=4 and n=8 diagnostics also fall below the 0.90
threshold and remain in the record.

The head can backtrack in real time, and earlier clock sectors
can contain probe information. The memory-only statement applies
to the complete response sector in the declared evolution. At
2T the tape returns to blank. The full prepared state recurs at
4T. Output lifetime and record retention are therefore finite.
The geometry, work, clean storage and sensory access are
explicitly priced. [Proof](PROOF.md) · [Resources](RESOURCES.md)
