## What the record represents

The memory begins in the same blank state for either hidden source. It
learns the sign of a calibrated phase response by discriminating a probe
state. The weak probe returns a noisy decision, never an exact preloaded
label. The two allowed dynamical responses, the correct layouts and the
probe-to-witness calibration are all part of the design. The source is a
conserved two-state spin. A classical binary latent-variable model with
trial error 1/4 reproduces the displayed colors, the classical pointer
frequencies, and their correlation with the terminal witness. Quantum
dynamics supplies one physical implementation of the acquisition.

The witness is independent of the head conditional on the source. Its
terminal outcome tests the prediction of a different interaction, but
the relationship between the two interactions is supplied. Likewise
the second task transfers the estimate to a new XOR challenge and
alternating layout. It uses the same bit and a supplied decoder. This
supports a functional record of one internal parameter within the
declared family. It leaves parameter dynamics, discovery of the law,
and acquisition of new response rules open.

## Access and discrimination

For exactly one allowed phase interaction the probe states have overlap
$\cos(2\theta)$. Their trace distance is $\sin(2\theta)$ in the studied
range. The best equal-prior discrimination or adaptive response score
is $(1+\sin(2\theta))/2$. The Y decoder attains it, including 0.75 in
the weak setting. Both final words require the same binary decision.
Their errors coincide. Scoring them as independent would give the
wrong bound.

The writer has no forward links back to the source once it enters
the response sector of the ordered map. The probe was reset by
dumping its old value, the challenge is independent of the source,
and both clock probabilities and large-size stage timings are
source-independent. The witness may contain a full bit about the
source, and D, E and the masks can retain useful information. Their
exclusion follows from their physical placement and the registered
readout algebra. With the main port and the response sector alone,
the accessible state factors through M even when late clock
coherences are retained. The direct port intentionally violates the
memory-only channel restriction by loading a source copy into A.

A reversible clock can backtrack. Its earlier position coherences need
a separate bound if an evaluator is given the whole clock. For
example, extend the phase angle to $\theta=\pi/2$ and coherently
superpose a before-query branch with an after-query branch. The two
post-query probe rays then coincide, but the two labels give opposite
relative phases between branches. A balanced coherent experiment
can distinguish those full states perfectly. This counterexample is
outside the registered angle range and shows why a reduced-memory
bound cannot be applied to arbitrary clock access. The D6 proof
limits this effect in the declared late window by the square root
of the unfinished-sector probability. A fresh physical intervention
that re-enters the station changes the protocol. [Proof](PROOF.md)

## Passive and direct nulls

The passive arm learns a record with the same quality, chooses an
independent fair decision, and produces the same overall distribution
of two-bank layouts for balanced b and fixed z. It loses the causal
advantage. In the direct null, a separate allowed coupling loads a
reference into A at the source. The same layouts and witness score
can be reproduced perfectly with a disabled M using this bypass.
The intended path is distinguished by its registered cut and
intervention contrast. Equal initial energy laws do not establish
identical accessible information channels.

The fixed route and stage rules, incoming clock localization, clean
registers, two fair coins and independent spin preparations are
supplied. The Hamiltonian is autonomous during a run. External
Schrödinger time, evaluation times and reversible recurrence are
part of the model. There is no dissipative reservoir, outside work
