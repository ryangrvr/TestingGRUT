# Sources and scope of novelty

The packet's finite-dimensional Hamiltonians, analytic calculations and
raw numerical outputs are supplied here in full. The methods use standard
quantum mechanics and matrix analysis. A GRUT-specific calculation or a
new test fixture is not a distinctive prediction about nature.

1. Sergey Bravyi, David P. DiVincenzo and Daniel Loss,
   [Schrieffer-Wolff transformation for quantum many-body systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0675),
   Annals of Physics 326, 2793–2826 (2011).
   Read the original paper's introduction and direct-rotation/effective
   Hamiltonian discussion. It distinguishes bare and exact low-energy
   spaces, develops effective Hamiltonians and linked cluster results,
   and explains the assumptions behind locality statements. It does not
   prove this packet's WM1 proposal is local. Our two-band module is
   solved exactly rather than by truncating a many-body SW series.

2. Tomasz Konopka, Fotini Markopoulou and Simone Severini,
   [Quantum Graphity: a model of emergent locality](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0861),
   Physical Review D 77, 104029 (2008).
   Primary-source context for dynamical graph proposals. The existence of
   a dynamical graph Hamiltonian, valence preference or conditional probe
   graph is not a uniquely GRUT idea. No claim of precedence is made for
   these broad ingredients.

3. Samuel A. Wilkinson and Andrew D. Greentree,
   [Geometrogenesis under Quantum Graphity: problems with the ripening Universe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07588),
   Physical Review D 92, 084007 (2015).
   The authors report disconnected configurations favored by the model
   they studied and investigate an additional graph term. This motivates
   treating connectivity and formation as separate tests; their numerical
   result is not evidence about CP-12 and is not generalized to all graph
   theories.
