# Frozen stiff-history consistency test

The original late-stiff \([1,40]\) bracket misses. The separate
\([0.5,1]\) extension freezes \(m=1\) meV,
\(\alpha=0.9333995236148092\), \(V_0/M^4=8.789272148529218\,10^{-11}\),
and \(r_{0.05}=0.0027647867171241764\). Keep the vacuum, \(a_{\rm end}\),
\(H_{\rm end}\), nominal radiation entropy at 200 GeV and fixed mass.
This is a candidate counterexample. Earlier BK18 and Planck marginals
are already known and cannot serve as reserved observations. No new
tensor or tilt comparison enters the consistency test.

## Declared backgrounds and limits

Test the instantaneous history for existence of a finite renormalized
produced stress. If it fails, preserve that result. Use two explicitly
prescribed regularizations for the physical sensitivity test, with
inflation-ending blend half widths of 0.06 and 0.15 e-fold and
reheating rates of 24 and 12 per e-fold, respectively. Match the
inflation and stiff \(a,H\) outside the ending blend. Use adiabatic
reheating with continuous conversion to the same asymptotic radiation
entropy. Specify the energy exchange, check positivity and
conservation, and compute the physical \(\epsilon_H=1\) pivot mapping
for each history. These prescriptions define effective backgrounds.

## Acceptance before data comparison

- Require a finite ultraviolet stress after state-independent
  subtraction, with no unbounded contribution hidden by a numerical
  cutoff. Separate local vacuum stress and produced stress. Pressure
  for deep subhorizon waves may be averaged over many oscillations
  within a Hubble time. Outside the short-wave limit, record the
  chosen tensor stress prescription and its domain.
- Require the vector and tensor energy and pressure fractions to stay
  below 0.01 in the spectator era through reheating and early
  radiation. Provide a time history or controlled envelope including
  the transitions. The vector is supposed to dominate the matter
  density later, so the early spectator threshold ends when the
  standard radiation-era matter fraction reaches 0.01.
- Require a conservative \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}<0.1\) from the amplified
  modes. Count all late hard vector energy as relativistic when
  making this bound. Use the radiation entropy, the actual tensor
  spectrum through the transition, and the horizon at BBN. Compare
  with a primary BBN or CMB radiation constraint separately.
- Resolve the tensor energy carrying range including modes with
  \(k>a_{\rm end}H_{\rm end}\). Refine the mode mesh and vacuum
  preparation. Require less than 5% quadrature and vacuum-start
  shifts in the integrated produced tensor energy. Bound the
  integrated missing ultraviolet energy below 5% of the computed
  hard-band energy or leave this gate open. Separate the soft vector
  and hard relativistic bands. The two smoothing choices must keep
  the rest density within 1% of the frozen fit and the radiation
  safety result must hold for both. Preserve any numerical miss.
- At equality require at least 99% of the rest density to have
  \(v<0.01\) and an upper estimate of comoving free streaming below
  \(0.01\) Mpc. Use the evolved spectrum including its positive tail.

If either background loses its spectator hierarchy, the corresponding
fixed-background fit fails. If a finite stress or a physical
radiation comparison depends on an uncontrolled tail, completion or
subtraction, leave it unresolved. Evaluate at most these two
regularizations and one mesh/start refinement for each. Keep the
reheating construction and the observational inference conditional.
