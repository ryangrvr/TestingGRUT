## Small evaluator ladder

1. **Exactly solvable rotating mass.** For $M=0.7$, signed momenta
   $p=\pm0.9$ and constant $\dot\theta/2=0.8$ on $0\le t\le4$,
   start in the negative free-energy state at $\theta=0$. Compare
   the analytic rotation to a direct complex ODE in the original
   mass-phase basis. At the same momentum construct a pair of
   Bloch states with equal $n=1/4$, energy and pressure, and opposite
   transverse components along the work axis. Compare their initial
   work. Require agreement below $10^{-9}$ for the rotating
   probability and below $10^{-12}$ for the matched moments.
2. **Independent spatial reference.** In Minkowski vacuum use the
   leading weak-source coefficient for
   $\theta=\theta_0 e^{-t^2/(2\tau^2)}\cos(2.4Mt)\cos(kz)$,
   with $M=1$. Use $k/M=1.0,1.6$ and $\tau M=12,24$.
   Evaluate the invariant two-body spectral integral and separately
   integrate the lab-frame momenta of both outgoing fermions with
   energy $E_1+E_2$ and total momentum $\mathbf k$. Use one
   Majorana species and its identical-particle factor. Require
   relative agreement below $10^{-6}$ and independently refine
   the lab quadrature. For the site-local comparison keep the same
   mean-square drive and evaluate the $k=0$ temporal response.
3. **Successful physical limits.** At $k=0$, with the same
   mean-square amplitude, the long pulse should give the vacuum
   pseudoscalar Majorana decay width. Compare $\tau M=24,48$
   and require less than 0.5% error at 48. Verify the collisionless
   volume derivative of the energy at fixed comoving populations
   and the massless $\rho a^4$ and $P/\rho$ limits. Use a finite
   normal-ordered two-level interaction to cross-check the pressure
   derivative including interaction and vacuum terms.
4. **Broken controls.** Omit the Majorana identical-particle factor,
   omit the interaction term in the pressure test and drop the
   spatial momentum from the weak-source kinematics. The first
   two should fail their reference tests. The third should predict
   nonzero secular production for the forbidden central frequency
   at $k/M=1.6$. The finite pulse has high-frequency tails. Require
   the exact finite-pulse energy there at $\tau M=24$ to be below
   $10^{-6}$ of the site-local result, while the allowed $k/M=1$
   case has at least 10% of the site-local result. A successful
   energy-only splice at the archived X11 boundary is also an
   intentionally insufficient acceptance rule.
