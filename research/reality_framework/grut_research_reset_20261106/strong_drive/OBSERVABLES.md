# Independent observable screen

The common target is the identity of the cold dark matter inferred from
cosmology. Use the mean density as calibration where indicated. A search
for a laboratory signal then tests a different observable.

| Mechanism | Preparation and remaining freedom | Calibration and independent test | Decision |

Other spectator models can produce the same type of perturbation. |
| M1 charged scalar composites with a trilinear Higgs portal | A formed, surviving population with specified charge distribution and a fixed portal profile. Portal mixing determines both recoil and hidden heating. | Use an independent mass and charge model and one recoil dataset for normalization. Suspend its forecast until the same population passes portal heating and formation. |
| Singlet scalar from Higgs-decay freeze-in | Dilute initial singlet in a radiation-dominated thermal SM. ## M1 cross-target test and event budget

For one dark mass, elastic isoscalar contact scattering and one isotope
per target, let $A$ denote the nuclear mass number. Up to known detector
response, the rate per detector mass has a common normalization
$\mathcal C$ and obeys

$$
\frac{dR_A}{dE_R}=\mathcal C A^2 |F_A(q)|^2 |F_X(q)|^2\eta(v_{\min}),
\qquad q=\sqrt{2m_AE_R},\qquad v_{\min}=\frac{q}{2\mu_A}.
$$

Here $\mu_A$ is the nucleus–dark matter reduced mass and
$\eta(v)=\int_{|\mathbf u|>v}f_{\rm lab}(\mathbf u)d^3u/|\mathbf u|$.
At matched $v_{\min}=v$, divide out $A^2|F_A|^2$ and take the
ratio of rates in two targets. The common normalization and halo
integral cancel, leaving $|F_X(2\mu_A v)|^2/|F_X(2\mu_B v)|^2$.
This equals one for pointlike contact scattering at the correct
mass. In data, account for the mass, isotope sums, recoil resolution,
backgrounds and alternative momentum-dependent interactions.
A broad composite charge distribution requires a population integral.
Composite form factors and cross-target recoil tests have precedents in
[Hardy et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05419) and
[Butcher et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01840).

For the archived $Q=2\times10^5$, $m_{\rm obj}=17.325$ TeV
trilinear-portal example, the recoil nodes at 34.11, 60.38 and 111.7 keV
in $^{131}$Xe, $^{74}$Ge and $^{40}$Ar all give $q\simeq91.24$ MeV
using $m_A\simeq A(0.93149\,\mathrm{GeV})$. Their minimum speeds
are about 113, 199 and 368 km/s. The common zero in momentum tests
that narrow population. The archived second peak is about $10^{-3}$
of the first and a charge spread $\sigma_{\ln Q}\sim0.3$ washes out
the node in the toy rate.

The portal-heating estimate supplies a cheap consistency screen.
With the background held fixed, recoil counts and leading portal
heating scale with the squared trilinear coefficient. At the
reference coefficient $2.7\times10^{-10}$ GeV the archived
estimates give 10 events in 200 tonne-years and added entropy
per charge $\Delta S/Q\simeq0.98$ or 2.5, depending on the thermal
treatment of Higgs mixing. The inherited budgets are 1.4 to 2.2.
Eliminating the coefficient at fixed preparation gives
$N_{\max}=10(S/Q)_{\rm budget}/(\Delta S/Q)_{\rm ref}$,
or about 6–22 events across the two treatments and budgets.
This sensitivity estimate depends on the archived profiles,
exposures and thermal history. Resolving a node with a secondary
rate of order $10^{-3}$ of the peak needs far better statistics.
The background-free xenon/argon shape comparison in the archive
is a separate, more optimistic test. Formation, the electroweak
crossover and the charge distribution enter before detector
backgrounds.

## Holder-free freeze-in control

Take a real stable $S$, $m_S=30$ GeV, with
$V\supset\lambda S^2H^\dagger H/2$. In the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann
decay approximation, use $x=m_h/T$ and
$H(T)=\sqrt{\pi^2g_*/90}\,T^2/M_*$.

$$
\frac{dY_S}{dx}
=\frac{2\Gamma(h\to SS)n_h^{\rm eq}}{sHx}\frac{K_1(x)}{K_2(x)},
\qquad
\Gamma(h\to SS)=\frac{\lambda^2 v^2}{32\pi m_h}
\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_S^2}{m_h^2}} .
$$

The factor two counts the daughters. Holding $g_*=g_{*s}=106.75$
constant gives the integral $\int_0^\infty x^3K_1(x)\,dx=3\pi/2$.
With $M_*=2.435\times10^{18}$ GeV, $m_h=125.25$ GeV and
$v=246.22$ GeV, $Y_S/\Gamma=4.63\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$.
Using $s_0\simeq2891\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$,
$\rho_c/h^2\simeq1.0537\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{GeV\,cm}^{-3}$
and $\Omega_ch^2=0.120$ fixes $Y_S\simeq1.46\times10^{-11}$.
Thus $\lambda\simeq2.73\times10^{-12}$ and the extra invisible
branching fraction is about $7.7\times10^{-21}$ for a 4.07 MeV SM
width. The calculation uses a zero-temperature decay width and
drops thermal thresholds, scatterings and backreaction. The
cited ATLAS 95% upper limit is 0.107. Cosmology fixed the portal
and the collider limit probes a separate observable with
insufficient sensitivity for this benchmark.

## QCD axion comparator

A field initially displaced from the temperature-dependent QCD
potential minimum obeys $\ddot a+3H\dot a+\partial_a V(a,T)=0$,
with $m_a^2(T)=\chi(T)/f_a^2$ near the minimum. The misalignment
angle can enter the abundance calibration and remains a
cosmological input. For DFSZ with $E/N=8/3$, chiral and lattice
inputs give

$$
m_a=5.70(6)(4)\,\mu\mathrm{eV}\,\frac{10^{12}\,\mathrm{GeV}}{f_a},
\qquad
g_{a\gamma\gamma}=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi f_a}\left(\frac83-1.92(4)\right).
$$

A 3.30 $\mu\mathrm{eV}$ example implies
$|g_{a\gamma\gamma}|\simeq5.0\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$
and a line near 798 MHz. ADMX reported a 90% exclusion of DFSZ
axions from 3.27 to 3.34 $\mu\mathrm{eV}$ for the standard halo
with all $0.45\,\mathrm{GeV\,cm^{-3}}$ of local dark matter in
axions. The example mass is chosen within the published interval
to illustrate an existing constraint. The coupling relation
predates the data. The exclusion is conditional on the local
density and distribution and does not identify the production
history.
