### 1. Definitions

The candidate is one specified operational quantum system,

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak G=(\mathcal A,\omega,H,\mathfrak I)} .
$$

The closed whole evolves under $H$. The access data $\mathfrak I$ give physical meaning to parts and experiments inside that whole. The dynamics then determine which parts interact, which correlations survive, and which information is sufficient for a specified effective prediction. Construction 1 below fixes every entry of $\mathfrak G$ and realizes these statements in one system.

$$
\mathfrak I=\big((\mathcal A_i)_{i\in L},(\mathcal K_R,\mathcal M_R)_{R\subseteq L}\big),
\qquad
\mathcal A_R=C^*\!\left(1,\bigcup_{i\in R}\mathcal A_i\right).

### 3. Consistency conditions

An admissible model has $\omega(1)=1$, $\omega(X^\dagger X)\ge0$, the Hamiltonian support condition, and the instrument normalization above. The same identity lies in all operational subalgebras. The site algebras generate $\mathcal A$ in this version. For disjoint regions declared independent at one time, their algebras commute. Other regions can share observables. Restrictions to overlapping algebras agree because they come from a single state.

For an allowed experiment with successive times $0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_n$ and instrument outcomes $x_1,\dots,x_n$, the probability is

$$
p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
=\omega\!\left(
\alpha_{t_1}\circ\mathcal J^{(1)}_{x_1}\circ\alpha_{t_2-t_1}
\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal J^{(n)}_{x_n}(1)
\right).
\tag{1}
$$

For adaptive experiments the later maps are chosen according to the earlier outcomes. The compositions in (1) are positive, and summing over the last outcomes replaces the last instrument by a unital channel. Iterating this argument gives normalization. Ignoring an intermediate outcome keeps the nonselective channel at that time. Omitting that operation is a different experiment. For a single outcome of positive probability, the updated state is $\omega(\mathcal J_x(\,\cdot\,))/\omega(E_x)$. This gives all histories and conditional updates from one parent.

Where an apparatus is represented explicitly, a finite instrument has the dilation

Measuring the apparatus outcome implements the declared maps on the original system. This gives consistency with unitary dynamics on a larger algebra. Its realization by the permitted interactions of a particular model is a separate physical condition. ### 4. Propositions

#### 4.1 Parts, autonomy, and the stationary obstruction

A **mathematical subsystem** is a subalgebra or a factor in a specified representation. A **physically accessible subsystem** has an algebra $\mathcal A_R$ together with the instruments and interactions declared on $R$. The restricted state $\omega|_{\mathcal A_R}$ is always defined. It gives the statistics of those instruments. Whether the available effects determine that state by tomography is a property of the access data.

In a full matrix algebra, commuting full matrix factors which jointly generate $\mathcal A$ give a tensor factorization. Their multiplication map from the tensor product is a surjective unital representation, and simplicity makes it faithful. A type-I factor $M_d\subset M_D$ in its usual representation acts as $M_d\otimes I_{D/d}$. Commutation alone is insufficient when algebras have centers, share gauge flux labels, or generate only part of the physical algebra. In those cases the candidate uses restricted states on the gauge-invariant algebras. It makes no automatic assignment of independent tensor factors to the regions. [Casini, Huerta and Rosabal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1183)

An **approximately autonomous subsystem** has a specified reduced channel $\Phi_t$ on its regional algebra, a class $\mathcal C$ of joint preparations, a time interval, and a tolerance. In the Heisenberg picture its condition is

$$
\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal C,\ t\in[0,T],\ E\in\mathscr E_R}
\big|\omega(\alpha_t(E))-(\omega|_{\mathcal A_R})(\Phi_t(E))\big|
\le\epsilon ,
$$

where $\mathscr E_R$ is the declared set of regional effects and $\Phi_t$ is unital and completely positive. The same channel must work for every preparation in $\mathcal C$. Exact autonomy for all states and all effects of $\mathcal A_R$ requires $\alpha_t(\mathcal A_R)\subseteq\mathcal A_R$, by separation of observables by states. For a full tensor factor and an interval with interior, this implies a split generator $H_R\otimes I+I\otimes H_{\bar R}$. Indeed the restricted derivation is inner, and subtracting its generator leaves an operator in the commutant. For a specified tensor split $H=H_R+H_{\bar R}+H_{\rm int}$ with embedded local terms, tracing the von Neumann equation gives

$$
\dot\rho_R=-i[H_R,\rho_R]-i\operatorname{Tr}_{\bar R}[H_{\rm int},\rho].
$$

The term from $H_{\bar R}$ vanishes by cyclicity of the partial trace. The interaction term can depend on joint correlations unavailable in $\rho_R$. A restricted state class can support approximate autonomy when its effect admits a common reduced channel. A **persistent object** has a stable identity test through its interactions, defined in Section 4.4. It can exchange information and energy while its identifying observables remain reliable.

**Stationary obstruction.** In finite $M_D$, a deterministic rule that is equivariant under conjugation cannot pick a concrete proper full quantum factor from a tuple of commuting Hermitian data, including $H$ and a commuting $\rho$. In their common eigenbasis every diagonal phase rotation fixes the input. Hence it must preserve the selected algebra. If that algebra has an off-diagonal element, phase averaging its orbit with the matching Fourier character isolates a matrix unit $|i\rangle\langle j|$. Its product with its adjoint gives a rank-one projector. The nonzero projections of a proper factor $M_a\otimes I_b$, $a,b>1$, all have rank a multiple of $b$. An algebra with only diagonal elements is commutative. These two cases exhaust the possibilities. An unordered finite set of such factors is also ruled out, since the connected phase torus fixes each member of an invariant finite set.

This proof concerns concrete factor selection from the specified stationary input. A spectral locality prior or an output defined up to equivalence changes the problem. The present candidate supplies the access structure directly. #### 4.2 Interaction and response

Let $\mathcal J,\mathcal J'$ be two permitted nonselective interventions on $R$. For a receiver effect $E$ at a later time, the response is

$$
\Delta_\omega(E,t)=
\omega\!\left[(\mathcal J-\mathcal J')(\alpha_t(E))\right].
\tag{2}
$$

Equation (2) follows by composing the intervention and readout in (1). An influence is detectable in the declared experiment class if this difference is nonzero for some admitted preparation and choice. If all states are admissible, a fixed choice is detectable on some state exactly when the self-adjoint operator in brackets is nonzero. If $\alpha_t(E)$ commutes with the intervention algebra, it commutes with every Kraus operator and the nonselective channel leaves it invariant. For permitted unitary kicks $e^{-i\eta B}$ at $R$, the derivative relative to the identity choice is

$$
\left.\frac{\partial\Delta_\omega(E,t)}{\partial\eta}\right|_{0}
=i\,\omega\!\left([B,\alpha_t(E)]\right).
$$

If all regional unitary kicks and all joint states are available, vanishing commutators with the intervention algebra are also necessary for absence of signalling to the specified readouts. A nonzero commutator then yields a kick and a state detecting it. With a narrower access table or state class, (2) is the criterion. Correlations can change conditional remote probabilities on selection of an outcome even when every nonselective choice leaves the remote readout invariant. A signal requires a controllable choice that changes the receiver's unconditioned probabilities.

For a tensor-product model define $\mathcal E_i$ by tracing out factor $i$ and reinserting its normalized identity. The mutually commuting Hilbert–Schmidt projections $\mathcal E_i$ give the unique centered support decomposition

$$
H_F=\left(\prod_{i\in F}(1-\mathcal E_i)\right)
    \left(\prod_{j\notin F}\mathcal E_j\right)H,\qquad H=\sum_F H_F .
\tag{3}
$$

A direct coupling between sites $i,j$ occurs when $(1-\mathcal E_i)(1-\mathcal E_j)H\ne0$. This condition is equivalent to a nonzero operator $[[H,E],B]$ for some $E$ at $j$ and $B$ at $i$. In fact if every such double commutator vanishes, $[H,E]$ acts trivially on factor $i$ for every $E$ on $j$. Since $\mathcal E_i$ commutes with left and right multiplication by $E$, $(1-\mathcal E_i)H$ then commutes with all of factor $j$. Its projection onto support on $j$ vanishes. Conversely if the joint support vanishes, each term is missing $i$ or $j$ and the double commutator vanishes. This operator is the term linear in $t$ in the small-kick response for disjoint sites.

Mediated response can occur at higher order. Draw an edge for each pair belonging to a nonzero support in (3). Each successive commutator with $H$ extends an operator's support by at most one step of this graph. For sites at graph distance $d$, all terms of degree less than $d$ in the commutator response vanish. Allowed operations, symmetries, and the state can suppress it further. The graph is a property of $H$ relative to supplied factors. It gives neither a metric in physical units nor relativistic causal structure.

#### 4.3 Internal clocks and conditional predictions

For a permitted joint readout, let $\{Q_c\}$ be the clock projection-valued readout and $\{E_x\}$ a readout commuting with it. At dynamical parameter $t$, put $\omega_t=\omega\circ\alpha_t$. Their joint and conditional probabilities are

$$
p(c,x;t)=\omega_t(Q_c E_x),\qquad
p(x\mid c;t)=\frac{\omega_t(Q_c E_x)}{\omega_t(Q_c)}\quad\text{if }\omega_t(Q_c)>0 .
\tag{4}
$$

Commutation makes the products joint effects. For a sequential measurement with noncommuting effects one instead uses the clock's instrument, with numerator $\omega_t(\mathcal J_c(E_x))$ and denominator $\omega_t(\mathcal J_c(1))$ for the immediate later read. A time delay inserts $\alpha_{\Delta t}$ before $E_x$. The conditional predictions refer to the chosen protocol.

A clock's **working interval** is an interval over which its calibration and correlations meet declared accuracy and support conditions. For an estimator $\hat t(c)$, the lab-parameter RMS error is $\sqrt{\sum_c\omega_t(Q_c)(\hat t(c)-t)^2}$. The relative prediction at a fixed $c$ may be exact even when this estimator is imprecise. Clock disturbance is the change of a later outcome distribution when the nonselective clock channel is inserted in (1). Stationary states can encode nontrivial conditional histories in (4). This is compatible with $\omega_t=\omega$ for all $t$, since the conditioning labels differ. The quantum law, clock observable and appropriate state are prerequisites. #### 4.4 Objects, records, and information use

To specify a **persistent object**, declare an identification test, a preparation class, a disturbance class, an interval and a tolerance. For a simple identification projector $Q\ne0,1$, preparations with $\omega(Q)\ge1-\epsilon_0$ retain the identification under unmeasured evolution with probability at least

$$
\omega(\alpha_t(Q))\ge 1-\epsilon_0-|t|\,\|[H,Q]\|.
\tag{5}
$$

Integrating $\partial_t\alpha_t(Q)=\alpha_t(i[H,Q])$ proves (5). A nonselective intervention with $\|\mathcal J(Q)-Q\|\le\delta$ adds at most $\delta$ to the error. Telescoping the adjoint maps gives the corresponding sum for multiple intervals and interventions since unital positive maps contract the norm of Hermitian operators. This bound concerns unconditioned probabilities. Conditional tests use (4) and their own support requirements.

A conserved identification can be exact. For a binding criterion, suppose an energy projector $B$ below a specified escape threshold satisfies $BQB\ge(1-\epsilon)B$. A state supported in $B$ stays supported there under $H$ and satisfies the persistence condition. Establishing the relation between the energy sector and $Q$ requires a binding model. Small transitions out of $Q$ can yield finite metastability. Maintained organization can also satisfy an interval bound when the restoring dynamics and its resource exchanges are included. These mechanisms are additional structure within the dynamics.

A **record** has a source outcome $s$, a pointer readout $r$, and a fixed decoder $f$. Use a joint readout if the source persists and commutes with the pointer, or use (1) for a source event in a specified earlier experiment. Reliability is $\Pr[f(r)=s]$, to be compared with the blind success $\max_s p(s)$. Its lifetime at tolerance $\epsilon$ is the longest interval after writing on which reliability stays at least $1-\epsilon$ for the stated disturbances and preparations. A record may use correlations of several carriers and can survive changes to other observables.

**Information use** requires an accessible dependence of a later action on the record. For a fixed action mechanism and a declared test effect, compare an informative preparation with a control changing the record correlation, or apply a permitted intervention to the record. Equation (1) attributes any difference to their different evaluations of the backward action effect. Matched marginals and matched energy distributions can exclude those variables as explanations of the observed contrast within the model. A control that disables the record-sensitive interaction tests its role separately. This definition concerns readouts and interventions. Life, autonomous agency, and subjective experience would require their own physical criteria.

### 6. Effective descriptions

#### 6.1 The criterion and the exact finite reduction

Fix admitted preparations $\mathcal C$, a time window, and a family $\mathscr P$ of experiments and readouts within that window. Each outcome $x$ in experiment $\pi$ has a backward effect $F_{\pi,x}\in\mathcal A$ obtained from the composition in (1). Thus $p_{\omega,\pi}(x)=\omega(F_{\pi,x})$. An effective state is a specified map $m$ on $\mathcal C$. It predicts the chosen experiments to tolerance $\epsilon$ if there are normalized predictions $\hat p_\pi(\cdot\mid m(\omega))$ with

$$
\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal C,\ \pi\in\mathscr P}
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(p_{\omega,\pi},\hat p_\pi(\cdot\mid m(\omega))\right)
\le\epsilon .
\tag{9}
$$

This is the quantitative meaning of emergence used here. It concerns a preparation class, readouts, interval and tolerance together. A stronger claim of closed effective dynamics requires an update on the retained states that realizes these predictions.

In a finite algebra, define the real linear space

$$
\mathcal V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
  \big(\{1\}\cup\{F_{\pi,x}:\pi\in\mathscr P,\ x\text{ an outcome}\}\big).
\tag{10}
$$

The restriction $\omega|_{\mathcal V}$ suffices exactly, since it evaluates every outcome effect. Two preparations are predictively equivalent for this family exactly when they agree on $\mathcal V$. Consequently any map $m$ is exact on $\mathcal C$ precisely when equality of its retained states implies equality on $\mathcal V$.

For all states, a summary by fixed linear expectations of operators with span $\mathcal W$ including $1$ must contain $\mathcal V$. If $\mathcal W$ misses a direction, there is a Hermitian matrix $D$ orthogonal to $\mathcal W$ under the trace pairing and with nonzero pairing with some $F_{\pi,x}$. It has trace zero. For sufficiently small $\eta$, the two density matrices $1/\operatorname{Tr}1\pm\eta D$ are positive and normalized. They agree on the summary and differ on that outcome. Thus $\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathcal V-1$ independent real expectations are sufficient and minimal for this kind of summary on the unrestricted state class. Restricted classes can need fewer.

For passive measurements of effects $E$ over any interval with interior, set $\mathcal L(X)=i[H,X]$. The same span is

$$
\mathcal V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
  \big(\{1\}\cup\{\mathcal L^n(E): n\ge0,\ E\text{ a target effect}\}\big).
\tag{11}
$$

One inclusion follows from $\alpha_t=e^{t\mathcal L}$ on the finite real vector space of Hermitian matrices. For the reverse, a real linear functional vanishing on the time-dependent readouts vanishes on their analytic continuation along the real axis and hence on every derivative at zero. Finite-dimensional duality gives equality. Iterated closure under $\mathcal L$ stops in at most $\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathcal A_{\rm sa}$ directions.

With interventions, (10) uses the actual branch maps and their ordered propagations. If $\mathcal V$ is invariant under the required propagators and branch maps, the reduced expectation functional updates within it. For a selected outcome $x$, the new functional at $B\in\mathcal V$ is $\omega(\mathcal J_x(B))/\omega(\mathcal J_x(1))$, at positive denominator. A fixed finite sequence can instead use a span indexed by how many steps remain. Adding controlled experiments can increase the span up to the full algebra, even when a small passive description works. Exact sufficiency alone gives neither a region with autonomous dynamics nor a cheap algorithm for obtaining and updating its sufficient expectations.

For approximate summaries, take the largest separation of two states given the same summary,

$$
\Delta_m=\sup_{m(\omega)=m(\omega'),\ \omega,\omega'\in\mathcal C}
   \ \sup_{\pi\in\mathscr P}\operatorname{TV}(p_{\omega,\pi},p_{\omega',\pi}).
$$

The triangle inequality forces $\epsilon\ge\Delta_m/2$ in (9). Using a representative state of each fiber gives error at most $\Delta_m$, without a promise of efficient computation. For clock-conditioned readouts the effects to retain include the joint numerator and the clock denominator. If each is approximated with error $\delta$, the exact denominator is at least $p_*>0$, and the approximate distribution has positive clock weight, the error in each conditional probability is at most $2\delta/p_*$. This follows by subtracting the two ratios and using that the approximate conditional probability lies in $[0,1]$. A small unconditional error can be inadequate for a rare clock reading.

### 7. Physical interpretation

#### 7.1 Thermodynamic accounting

Under the adopted unmeasured evolution, the full density spectrum and $\omega(H)$ are constant. Hence the total von Neumann entropy is constant, using $S(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\ln\rho)$. For an actual tensor split, $S_A+S_B=S_{AB}+I(A:B)$, where $I$ is the quantum mutual information. Marginal entropy changes can therefore record the formation or loss of correlations. If constraints prevent the tensor split, regional entropies require the choice of physical algebra and its treatment of the center.

In Construction 1 the ready clock, signal and Bell blank provide order at the boundary. After writing, the memory marginal entropy is one bit, the source-memory mutual information is two bits, and the joint readout of source and parity contains one bit. The conditioned work state is pure in the unmeasured run. Reading and ignoring an outcome discards correlations with the apparatus, and conditioning on a selected outcome describes a subensemble. A trace, pinching map, or conditional update describes a reduced or selected ensemble. A unitary account including the apparatus preserves the joint entropy. For Construction 1, $e^{-2\pi iH/\Omega}=-1$ on the whole Hilbert space, so the joint density returns after a full cycle. This construction supplies an oriented preparation for writing. It adopts no universal thermodynamic arrow.

A reservoir channel has a conditional derivation. If a subsystem $M$ and bath $B$ begin in $\rho_M\otimes\tau_B$, tracing out $B$ after the joint unitary defines a channel on $M$. An initially correlated bath requires checking whether different allowed joint preparations with the same $\rho_M$ lead to the same predictions, by (9). A time-local dissipative equation or repeated reset requires an additional approximation and accounts for the resources that maintain the bath.

For example, a finite Gibbs bath $\tau_B=e^{-\beta_B H_B}/\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta_B H_B}$ with inverse temperature $\beta_B>0$ and the product start above obeys the exact entropy balance

$$
\beta_B Q_B=-\Delta S_M+I(M:B)_{\rm final}
                  +D(\rho_B^{\rm final}\Vert\tau_B),
\qquad
Q_B=\operatorname{Tr}\big[H_B(\rho_B^{\rm final}-\tau_B)\big].
$$

Indeed global entropy conservation gives $\Delta S_M+\Delta S_B=I_{\rm final}$, and expansion of the relative entropy $D$ gives $\beta_B Q_B=\Delta S_B+D$. This explains the minimum bath heat cost of a reset within the stated preparation and unitary protocol. A controller or work system used in a physical reset must be included in its own ledger.
