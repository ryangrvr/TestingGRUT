## Energy and memory

The writing law is time independent. The source bit commutes with the
Hamiltonian, the carrier exchanges energy with the core, and the full
state evolves unitarily. At no point is a failed transmission discarded to
increase fidelity. Numerical storage following the finite scattering
calculation neglects the measured residual gate overlap; this is a stated
approximation, not an exact infinite-time factorization.

The protected record is one redundantly encoded classical bit. The source
and low-entropy blank state are initial resources. Neither information nor
organized matter is claimed to arise from nothing. Bare formation was
tested separately and did not establish a durable record.

## The matched control and the writer

With the same low transverse amplitude everywhere, F=0.05, the finite Ising
register retains the prepared bit better and gives nearly the same matter
readout. Thus the demonstrated retention does not establish a benefit from
the proposed facilitation law. The old F=1 comparison changes the field
strength as well as its neighbor dependence.

The carrier crosses each gate in 2w/(p0/M)=0.003. In the fast straight-line
limit, its conditional pulse area is Omega*w/(p0/M)=pi/2, giving a controlled
flip. This leading operation does not require facilitation. The reported
finite-clock scattering probabilities belong to M1-W; they have not been
recomputed for the different uniform-floor core. The writer is a programmed
apparatus, not evidence that facilitation autonomously organizes records.

No equilibrium memory phase or self-correction follows from L=3–5 pure-state
records. A future proposed correction cascade would have to specify noise,
an error syndrome, correction dynamics, entropy/energy resources and an
advantage over this matched baseline. No such calculation has been executed.

## KMS and fluctuation–dissipation

For the finite core, \(\rho_\beta\propto e^{-\beta H_{M1}}\) obeys KMS.
The spectral weights satisfy detailed balance; in units \(\hbar=1\),
\(S(-\omega)=e^{-\beta\omega}S(\omega)\). The usual equilibrium FDT
continues to hold. It is not a rule for the four-point growth exponent.
Thus an OTOC is a different observable from linear response; it does not
make fluctuation–dissipation disappear.

The written-record scattering preparation is not a Gibbs state. Closed
unitarity does not require every initial state to be thermal, and a
stationary Gibbs ensemble does not prove that every prepared state rapidly
thermalizes.

## Why this does not yet modify gravitational chaos

The distinction can be stated algebraically. Consider a family of chaotic
Hamiltonians \(H_r\) and a record algebra with orthogonal projectors
\(P_r\). The closed law \(H=\sum_rP_r\otimes H_r\) retains every
\(P_r\), permits the record to affect interactions, and can scramble
within each \(H_r\). It admits a finite Gibbs state. Structured memory
therefore does not by itself bound the largest exponent of the other
operators. M1's exactly saturated subspace is a concrete version of this
distinction.

There is a further obligation if the earlier Einstein sector is retained.
In the usual semiclassical near-horizon calculation, graviton exchange
gives an eikonal contribution proportional to \(G_Ns\), while the
relative boost gives \(s\propto e^{2\pi Tt}\). A memory sector that
changes a coefficient without removing this channel does not lower its
exponent. These assumptions and their stringy qualifications are already
discussed in MSS, section 3; see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

A gravitational successor must specify which horizon/scattering assumption
its new law changes, calculate a regulated gravitational observable, and
recover the required low-energy gravitational tests. An uncalculated
inequality is not substituted for that law. This candidate does not meet
those obligations and does not infer that no candidate ever could.
