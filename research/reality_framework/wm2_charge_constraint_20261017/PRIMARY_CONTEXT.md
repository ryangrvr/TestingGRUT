# Primary-source context and provenance

The Gauss generators, link shifts and minimally coupled hopping used as
building blocks are standard Hamiltonian gauge-theory constructions.
Emonts and Zohar's [*Gauss Law, Minimal Coupling and Fermionic PEPS for
Lattice Gauge Theories*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01294), sections 2.1-2.3,
write the link shift, charged hopping and conserved Gauss operators
explicitly and distinguish a constraint from an equation of motion.
Those examples start on a supplied lattice; they do not derive WM2's
extra flux-support constraint or its event metric.

For the broader Hamiltonian construction and finite-dimensional
simulation context, see Zohar, Cirac and Reznik, [*Quantum Simulations
of Lattice Gauge Theories using Ultracold Atoms in Optical Lattices*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02312).
The gauge-theory framework is prior physics, not a distinctive GRUT
prediction. Our seven-level partial shifts and exact kernel are defined
and checked directly rather than borrowing an assertion of universality
from that literature.

The new in-program content is the bounded WM2-UC1 construction, its
full-history charged-position estimate, exact vacuum formation isometry,
and the internal-channel obstruction in the same frozen class. The
commutant-product identity is elementary operator algebra; no priority
claim is made for it or for gauge-invariant nonlocal interactions.

All upstream research inputs are pinned to their repository bytes.

not treated as external validation. These derivations, implementation
