# Primary-source context

These comparisons bound the novelty claim. IG1 is a scoped classification
and a moving-boundary application, not a discovery that local interactions
can have propagation bounds.

* H. Wilming and A. H. Werner, [Lieb–Robinson bounds imply locality of
  interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10062), Phys. Rev. B 105, 125101
  (2022), Theorem 2. Their fixed tensor-lattice, finite-body converse constructs
  an equivalent local potential from short-time bounds. Our elementary
  double-commutator necessity test is consistent with that established result.
  We do not import their tensor-lattice hypotheses into WM2's changing graph.
* B. Nachtergaele, R. Sims and A. Young, [Quasi-Locality Bounds for Quantum
  Lattice Systems, Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02428), J. Math. Phys. 60,
  061101 (2019), Theorems 3.1 and 3.3. Their framework controls dynamics from
  spatial interaction norms and handles on-site energies separately. IG1's
  finite-prefix nested-commutator proof uses a simpler uniform total norm on
  fixed few-carrier sectors. That norm cannot replace a local interaction
  norm at general finite density.

The additional WM2 obligation is the common-space comparison to the complete
moving-end geometry and receiver. SUFFICIENT_RULE.md writes that step explicitly
instead of citing a fixed-graph theorem as if it controlled geometry changes.
