We then derived a microscopic ingredient of the requested effective theory:

## Exact object being calculated

Fix a graph G. Its true compression is

    H_G = P_G H12 P_G = D_G + t V_G,
    D_G = kappa Q + 2m N_vertex + (epsilon_E/2) sum E_leg^2
          + a sum_(e in G) R_e,
    a = Gamma (M-Q)/M.

R_e projects onto a removable neutral edge. Off-diagonal graph changes are
excluded by this declared compression, but their diagonal cost is retained.
It is NOT the full joint graph Hamiltonian. Omitting the last term instead
gives the separately labelled bare fixed-graph comparator a=0.

Let P project onto states with all edge fermions filled and all vertex
fermions empty, allowing arbitrary divergence-free electric flux. Let C=I-P
be the charged subspace. The exact energy-dependent Feshbach operator is

    H_eff(E) = P H_G P + P H_G C (E-C H_G C)^(-1) C H_G P.

Take the reference energy E0=kappa Q+aQ and the neutral electric vacuum |0>.
For a simple oriented cycle c, W_c shifts its 2l electric half-legs by +1,-1
around the cycle and leaves the fermions unchanged. We calculate

    <W_c 0| H_eff(E0) |0> = K_l(a) t^(2l) + O_G(t^(2l+2)).

This is a well-defined off-diagonal effective response. It is NOT a
coordinate-independent standalone observable, an energy-independent effective
Hamiltonian valid for every flux background, or the complete vacuum-energy
coefficient. The exact eigenvalue problem also needs E self-consistency,
normalization, diagonal terms and couplings between flux states.

## Minimal paths and the sign

The desired final flux changes each of 2l distinct half-legs once. Each hop
changes only one half-leg. Consequently fewer than 2l hops cannot generate it.
At that order every hop is on the selected cycle, even if the graph has
additional edges and spectator vertices. Every proper intermediate state is
charged: a proper subset of a simple oriented cycle cannot carry a closed
nonzero flux with all matter occupations restored.

After j hops, let n_v(j) be the number of occupied vertices and r(j) the
number of touched edges. Its excitation denominator at E0 is exactly

    D_j = 2m n_v(j) + (epsilon_E/2) j - a r(j).

There are no electric cutoffs in this minimal-path calculation: each touched
field is 0 or plus/minus1. At the frozen parameters 0<=a<.1 and epsilon_E/2=.125,
all denominators are positive. The coefficient is

    K_l(a) = sum_(Pauli-allowed orderings) product_(j=1)^(2l-1) D_j^(-1) > 0.

The sign can be proved independently of enumerating paths. The edge-end
orientation factors multiply to (-1)^l. The closed permutation of l fermions
contributes (-1)^(l-1). Their product is -1. The 2l-1 negative resolvents supply
another -1. All allowed minimal histories therefore add with positive sign.
The reverse loop has the same coefficient.

Lengths3–6 are evaluated with exact rational arithmetic; lengths7–9 use
floating-point sums over subsets, with exact integer ordering counts.
`loop_paths.py` also evaluates a=.06, the compressed shift at N=6,Q=6.
This auxiliary shift on cycle controls represents a possible embedding,
not the actual a for every isolated cycle size.

A positive hopping matrix element is consistent with the sign of a +K(W+W†)
term in this vacuum neighborhood. It is tempting to call this a pi-flux
preference. That must not be promoted to a global flux ground-state theorem:
the electric Hamiltonian is nonzero, the kernel is energy dependent, and its
coefficients change with the background flux. No classical pi flux was imposed.

## Independent matrix elimination and a controlled domain

`feshbach_check.py` constructs the frozen Gauss-law basis on triangles and
squares at electric cutoffs L=1,2,3. It eliminates the entire charged block,
not just the minimum paths. Its exact matrix-series coefficient agrees with
the path sum within 1e-12. It also directly solves the charged resolvent at
hopping .02,.04,.08,.16 for a=0 and .06: 48 checks. At hopping .02 the largest
relative correction to the leading coefficient is .00137502. Linear-solve
residuals are below 1e-10. See feshbach_results.json.
`embedding_check.py` independently checks the square coefficient inside a
square with a diagonal (two triangle shortcuts) and a square with a pendant
edge, using their actual compression shifts. Both full-basis coefficients
agree with the same minimum-path formula within 1e-12.

A sufficient finite-graph convergence condition is explicit. Charged diagonal
gaps obey D_C>=delta=2m+epsilon_E/2-a because every nonneutral edge has at
least one unit of squared electric field, and a<epsilon_E/2. Also ||V_C||<=2Q.
Hence the resolvent Neumann series converges when

    r=2Q |t|/delta < 1.

The norm remainder after retaining resolvent powers through k is at most
(delta^-1) r^(k+1)/(1-r), before multiplying the two source couplings.
This is conservative but rigorous in the stated finite graph problem.
It does not provide a uniform large-N expansion at frozen t=1. The numerical
controls are inside this sufficient regime. Deriving a uniform linked-cluster
bound or a nonperturbative computation is still required for the requested
large-geometry free-energy ranking.

## Rewiring changes the degree coefficient too

The same compression gives a one-pair denominator delta=Delta-a, not Delta.
The fourth-order vacuum energy becomes

    E_G = kappa Q+aQ - 2Q t^2/delta
          + [4Q+2 sum_i binom(d_i,2)] t^4/delta^3 + O_G(t^6).

`compressed_degree.py` checks this using full-basis resolvents on a path,
star, square and triangle-plus-isolate, with their actual a values.
The degree-regularizing sign survives. This correction does not distinguish
matched four-regular competitors.

## Consequence for the supplied torus competitors

At fixed N,Q=2N the compressed a is identical for square and thin tori.
Each has N square cycles, so their leading square-loop vertices are equal.
At N=729, a=181/1820 and K_4(a)=.0960409640774.

The thin 9x81 torus has 81 length-nine winding cycles. Their one-flux response
first occurs at t^18 with K_9(a)=.0132499634076. The balanced27x27 torus's
shortest winding cycles have length27 and first occur at t^54.
The tables for all supplied sizes are in geometry_channels_results.json.
These coefficients distinguish winding channels without supplying a geometric
reward. They do not determine which entire graph has lower free energy.

Why not square the vertex and declare an answer? A two-excursion contribution
via the plus/minus one-flux states would be -2 K_l(a)^2 t^(4l)/[l(epsilon_E-a)].
Other diagonal charged paths, energy dependence and flux couplings contribute
at relevant orders as well. Indeed at a=0 the triangle channel alone would
contribute about -.0268245 t^12 per triangle; two such channels account for
only about .0536490 of the previously computed ring6-minus-two-triangles
coefficient .1513864. The loop vertex is an ingredient, not the whole energy.

Random regular graphs also have cycle-dependent channels. Their shorter
charge paths or differing cycle counts cannot by themselves rank the full
energy. We have not computed a matched free energy for the large random,
thin and balanced competitors and do not report one.

## What this advances

There is now a derived, sign-resolved gauge-flux interaction to use in the
next effective theory, alongside the previously derived degree penalty.
The local/global obstruction explains exactly why keeping only the square
term would be insufficient. The next calculation needs diagonal connected
clusters, winding sectors, interactions among loops, and graph mixing under
a common state prescription; it cannot replace the Hamiltonian by a graph
Laplacian spectrum or a fitted loop reward.

The canonical finite-temperature entropy obstruction is already proved for
full CP12 in attempt12/THERMAL_THEOREM.md. Nothing here evades it. Ground-state
and prepared nonequilibrium questions remain distinct.

## Priority and reproduction

Generating gauge-loop terms by fermion hopping expansions is established
lattice-gauge physics; see M. Hasenbusch, Phys. Rev. D97,114512 (2018),
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.114512, and C. Gattringer,
https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9903021 . Their Hamiltonians and coefficients
are not CP12's. The results here are model-specific calculations, not a
claim to have discovered induced gauge theory.
