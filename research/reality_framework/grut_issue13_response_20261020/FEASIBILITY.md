retrospective exclusion test. This response compares that baseline with the
new wall proposal before treating it as an empirical program. No large new
production or expanding-network campaign is authorized by a favorable fit.

The six replays are a bounded prerequisite check: they ask whether the
already tested cold preparations demonstrate negative-vacuum domains. They
do not. It is therefore unwarranted to assert that core formation itself
has already excluded this preparation through walls. A genuine thermal
transition ensemble is a different initial-history specification, even
while leaving the action unchanged.

For a thermal proposal, a finite chemical potential can change the phase
structure. Thus the neutral planar wall calculation itself needs no yield,
but the relevance of a thermal network to a charged preparation cannot be
declared yield-independent before demonstrating that the charge is negligible
or accounting for it. Visible reheating is not hidden thermalization.

Likewise, a subdominant-network CMB template cannot simply be multiplied
to the enormous benchmark amplitude if the walls would dominate the
background before recombination. One must test that history with expansion
and backreaction, or justify a conservative rejection from an earlier
observable. A calculated microscopic collapsing bubble is not automatically
constrained by a late horizon-scale network bound.

Minimum next deliverable from the thermal/network side: specify one history
and all free inputs, calculate its transition/formation and persistence, and
identify an applicable observable whose magnitude is fixed before inspecting
new residuals. If those inputs stay free, publish conditional constraints on
history. Do not call them a predicted relic abundance or empirical validation.
