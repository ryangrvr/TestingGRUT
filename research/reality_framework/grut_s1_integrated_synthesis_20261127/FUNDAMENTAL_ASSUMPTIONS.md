# Assumptions and what follows

The roles of a premise are recorded in the table. A mathematically supplied premise can be well supported experimentally. A calculated consequence can be exact and still depend on a model whose realization has not been shown. The first category gives the starting framework. The remaining categories are choices needed for particular physical comparisons.

| Premise | How it enters the working account | Consequence that can be derived | Independent content still needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One physically self-contained system for a closed calculation | Modeling boundary and philosophical premise of the program. The physical specification contains any relevant reservoirs and instruments. | Joint probability and marginal compatibility follow given the state and instrument rules. | Choice of global theory, measurable model boundary, state measure and dynamical constraints. |
| State positivity, algebra and Born probabilities | Adopted quantum kinematics and probability rule, independently tested in their usual domains. Commutators and $\hbar$ enter the representations or matching. | Positive effects, instrument probabilities, distinguishability, entropic inequalities and unitary invariants. | Physical choice of algebra and representation, origin of $\hbar$, and any solution of outcome selection. |
| Reversible microscopic dynamics | Supplied unitary or constrained dynamics in the quantum examples. Some parent tracks instead earn a classical deterministic substrate in a narrower model. | Exact evolution and restrictions, conditional observables and effective dynamics under controlled reductions. | Selection of a generator, unique quantum lift, causal structure or new physical law. |
| Observable access, clocks and physical reference agreements | Specify instrument algebras, measurements, gauge reductions and resource limits. | Exact matched probability agreement in complete F5 charts, and failure of comparisons when the physical event or access changes. | Gauge consistency in general gravity and a physical choice of clocks, preparing apparatus and measured effects. |
| Locality, Lorentzian dimension and signature | Adopted or matched in local lattice and relativistic EFT regimes. CP-12 and the older programs test potential derivations separately. | Lieb–Robinson bounds in stated local Hamiltonian classes, causal cones or effective signals under equations and conditions. | A mechanism that derives physical locality and a relativistic continuum from a physically warranted parent. |
| General relativity and matching of $G$ | Adopted in coupled and matter EFT examples. Diffeomorphism invariance is a separately stated property. | Conservation laws and classical constraints, weak metric response and specific propagation theorems. | Selection of the gravitational action and an anomaly-controlled quantum completion. |
| Gauge group, representations, content and couplings | Specified SM/Dirac or additional fields in the EFT tests. Empirical matching fixes parameters in physical applications. | Anomaly constraints, exact charges conditional on representations, spectral and EFT matching predictions. | Selection of the measured laws and couplings by an independent principle. F4 excludes one qualitative selection inference at its strength. |
| Finite information, reservoir state and preparation | Supply finite capacities, ready memory, thermal or nonthermal reservoir, input state, clocks and work budgets case by case. Capacity is distinct from spatial compactness. | Bounded repair windows, information-discrimination limits and thermodynamic costs. | Physical source, costs and preparation of a device or cosmological state. Maximum mixing, a low-entropy preparation and topology are independent hypotheses. |
| Cosmological background, vacuum term and primordial history | Supply geometry, $\rho_\Lambda$, initial perturbations or fields, and their matching to a late population. | Conditional evolution or tests against cosmological data. | A unique or empirical state selection, controlled production and the observed vacuum value. |
| Scoring and physical identity | Supply registers, criteria, access and task horizon. There can be competing definitions tied to different measurements. | Exact or modeled contrasts for information-dependent function and restoration. | A bridge to arbitrary organizations, natural realizations and subjective experience. |

**Established physical restrictions** include conservation, channel positivity under the relevant open-system premises, quantum statistical bounds, Ward identities, anomalies and appropriate effective-field-theory matching conditions. Their hypotheses matter. Renormalization group transport relates matched theories across scales and cannot generally determine its own independent boundary conditions. No tested additional closure, maximum order, spectrum, global phase symmetry, or responsiveness axiom selects the complete physical world.
