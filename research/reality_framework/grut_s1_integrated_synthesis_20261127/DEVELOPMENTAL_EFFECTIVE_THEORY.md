# Developmental effective theory

D1–D7 and I1–I3 establish how physical records can affect later physical work in specified models. The operative quantities are joint preparation, separated access, the interactions that write and use information, and the resource budget. The models have different substrates and schedules. A common description of their records and tasks does not establish that their Hamiltonians arise from a single microscopic process.

## Operational definitions

- **Order** is scored by a declared configuration criterion, expectation, shape class or task output. The initial preparation is a separate input. D2 counts either uniform orientation. D3 and D4 require the actual earlier label. D7 scores quantum entanglement fidelity.
- **Record** is accessible statistical dependence on a physical event, condition or state variable. A record's retention, its information, and the physical use of that information can be measured separately. Equality of visible marginals can conceal distinct usable correlations.
- **Historical restoration** is success on an output linked to the earlier physical label. In an unknown-label task the best restoration cannot beat the best discrimination available from the complete physical input.
- **Development** is generation of a physical configuration via a rule, growth path or environment-dependent process. In the examples, ready matter, a local rule or actuator, and the allowed observation remain part of the preparation. Completion can depend on a prior internal record without requiring a preloaded copy of the outcome.
- **Adaptive prediction** is use of acquired information to improve performance on a later, distinct test whose outcome depends on a hidden condition. A constant or directly supplied answer is a control, as is a passive record that is never used.
- **Task value** $V_{\mathcal T}$ is the supremum over admissible operations of a declared score. A record can enlarge that set, but changes in work, timing or reset can also reduce the attainable net value under a common budget. A lost interface or hostile intervention can lower the score of a chosen policy without lowering the optimum for the larger information class.
