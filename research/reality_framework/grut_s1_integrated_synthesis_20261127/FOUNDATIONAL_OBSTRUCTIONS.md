# Foundational obstructions and physical underdetermination

## The common mathematical statement

Fix a class $\mathfrak T$ with its stated structures and quotient by its genuine representation redundancies, such as gauge transformations. Let $\mathfrak A\subseteq\mathfrak T$ be the theories that satisfy the named admissibility conditions. A declared observation protocol gives a response map $R:\mathfrak A\to\mathcal D$. The map may include calibrated access, preparation and instrument effects. A proposed physical target $L:\mathfrak A\to\mathcal L$ can be inferred from these data exactly when it is constant on every fiber of $R$ relevant to the claim. Proof. If $L=f\circ R$, members with the same response have the same target. Conversely, if the target is constant on each fiber, define $f$ on the image of $R$ by that common value. For complete reconstruction, $L$ is the identity on the physically distinct classes. This is the standard identifiability condition.

With compatible calibrated probes on the same $\mathfrak A$, the joint fiber is the intersection of the component fibers for the observed data. Adding such a probe cannot enlarge the set of solutions with the original data. An additional consistent restriction can also remove members without deriving an empirical value. To support law selection in the stronger sense, an independently supported condition must constrain physical alternatives without imposing the target data as a premise. Different unitary coordinates for one state and its instruments label the same description. Two different models which happen to produce the same limited data can remain physically inequivalent and testable elsewhere.

This result is a way of stating the burden of proof. F1–F5 do not share one $\mathfrak T$ or one observation map. Their witnesses cannot be combined into a single surviving member of an intersection of all physically motivated constraints. In particular, a model might evade one obstruction by supplying data or restricting dynamics that its original class did not include.

### Why the cyclic boundary matters

For arbitrary $U_0,\ldots,U_{N-2}$ and chosen state $\psi_0$, build $|\Psi\rangle=N^{-1/2}\sum_t |t\rangle U_{t-1}\cdots U_0|\psi_0\rangle$, with the empty product at $t=0$. The cyclic shift includes $U_{N-1}=(U_{N-2}\cdots U_0)^\dagger$, hence has identity holonomy around the orbit. The history is a fixed state for every original list. The full F5 lift supplies two interacting reference factors, a physical effect algebra and noisy readout instruments in which this freedom still holds. Fixing a different wrap independently of the unitaries could constrain the possible initial state or net unitary. Its effect would come from that independent physical boundary. [F5 foundations](../grut_f5_closure_consistency_20261127/MATHEMATICAL_FOUNDATIONS.md)

The cases locate different failures. F1 concerns limited inverse reconstruction. F2 shows that distinct physical interventions can resolve some ambiguities. F3 shows dynamical freedom at matched background data and consistency conditions. F4 shows empirical selection despite underdetermined symmetry. F5 shows that agreement between descriptions of one world does not select which of many dynamical worlds is realized. The only cross-campaign generalization earned here is the identifiability statement on properly specified classes and data. No universal obstruction to deeper necessity follows.
