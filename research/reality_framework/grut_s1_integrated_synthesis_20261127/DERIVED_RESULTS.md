## Finite information, observation and adaptive response

**Reversible restoration with a record.** D1 maps $\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle$ on S and blank M,A to $|0\rangle_S(\alpha|00\rangle+\beta|11\rangle)_{MA}$ by the three stated controlled flips. Orthogonal input branches survive on MA and their inner products are preserved. The intermediate gate makes later action physically dependent on the record. This is fixed-reset dynamics with provided resources. [Recorded D1 calculation](../grut_i1_integrated_20261125/EVIDENCE_MAP.md)

**Restoration bounded by accessible distinguishability.** Suppose the historic label is $h$ with prior $\pi_h$ and available density $\rho_h$. A recovery channel followed by its success measurement induces a POVM $E_h$ on the input through the channel adjoint. Its success is at most the optimal discrimination score $\max_{\{E_h\}}\sum_h\pi_h\operatorname{tr}(\rho_h E_h)$. Two physically identical available states with equal priors give at most half. For $d$ equally likely alternatives whose only distinguishing input is a $D$-dimensional register, $\rho_h\le I$ gives an upper bound $\min(1,D/d)$ by summing $\operatorname{tr} E_h$ over labels. This does not count the distinguishable outputs needed to preserve unitarity while storing errors. [D4 theorem and syndrome storage](../grut_d4_internal_recognition_20261124/INFORMATION_BOUND.md)

**Optimal binary acquisition.** For equal priors, the optimal decision score is $\tfrac12+\tfrac14\|\rho_0-\rho_1\|_1$. D6's pure assay overlap $\cos(2\theta)$ at the declared angles gives $(1+\sin(2\theta))/2$, attained by its Y decoder. A second response can legitimately use the same bit without making its errors statistically independent of the first response. [D6 proof](../grut_d6_adaptive_20261125/PROOF.md)

**Quantum programme-assisted preservation.** D7 supplies probabilities $\pi_b$, fixed diagonal phase factors, and programme states $\rho_b$ conditionally independent of the Bell payload. With $C=\sum_b\pi_b e^{-2i(-1)^b\phi}\rho_b$, Stinespring's representation bounds the recovery contribution of the coherence block by $\|C\|_1$. A controlled polar factor of $C$ saturates it, with each population preserved on the scored output. Thus $F_{\rm opt}=(1+\|C\|_1)/2$. Tracing the programme out gives $(1+|\operatorname{tr}C|)/2$ under the same phase model. The I1 extension with common population relaxation follows by bounding that part separately and using the same polar recovery. These optima concern a specified interface and task. [D7 proof](../grut_d7_functional_recovery_20261125/PROOF.md) · [I1 proof](../grut_i1_integrated_20261125/DERIVATIONS.md)

**Control with matched resources.** Access to a record cannot decrease the optimum if the larger policy class can implement every blind policy with the same resources. At fixed control menus, replacing an input density by a different density on the same accessible degrees changes any bounded normalized score by at most the input trace distance. Taking the supremum of each score preserves that bound. This uses contractivity and the effect characterization of trace distance. It does not imply that building, retaining or reading the record is feasible at the same work cost. I2 and I3 make those constraints physical, and I2 finds no positive net gain at the optimum of its frozen menu, and I3 gives a positive restricted comparison. [I1 decision comparison](../grut_i1_integrated_20261125/INTEGRATED_THEORY.md) · [I2 costs](../grut_i2_persistence_20261125/THERMODYNAMICS.md) · [I3 costs](../grut_i3_predictive_memory_20261126/THERMODYNAMICS.md)

**Predictive sign from a switching source.** For a symmetric two-state continuous-time Markov source with flip rate $q$, the mean sign conditional on its value $\tau$ earlier decays by $e^{-2q\tau}$. A past observed sign with independent symmetric error $\epsilon$ then predicts the present sign with probability $1/2+(1/2-\epsilon)e^{-2q\tau}$. I3 adds separate acquisition, record decay, reaction and work costs. The signal correlation alone is insufficient for the reported task advantage.
