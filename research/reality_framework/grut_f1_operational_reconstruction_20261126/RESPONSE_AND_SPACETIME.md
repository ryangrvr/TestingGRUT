# Operational response and spacetime

## Retarded data and their information content

Take a specified observable algebra, stationary state $\rho$,
and dynamics generated by $H$. For a perturbation
$-\sum_b f_b(t) A_b$ and a readout $A_a$ the first-order
change is the retarded convolution with
$$
\chi_{ab}(t)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\Theta(t)
   \operatorname{Tr}\rho[A_a(t),A_b(0)],
\qquad A_a(t)=e^{iHt/\hbar}A_a e^{-iHt/\hbar}.
$$
This is the Kubo formula for the displayed source sign. The time
parameter and the source and detector algebras already have
operational meanings. In the algebraic form, replace the trace
by a state evaluated on the commutator. [Kubo](https://doi.org/10.1143/JPSJ.12.570)

For a finite stationary model choose a common eigenbasis of $H$
and $\rho$ with energies $E_m$ and populations $p_m$.
The unsymmetrized correlation spectrum for
$\langle A_a(t)A_b\rangle$ has weights
$2\pi p_m (A_a)_{mn}(A_b)_{nm}$ at
$\omega=(E_n-E_m)/\hbar$, with Fourier convention $e^{i\omega t}$.
The commutator spectrum replaces $p_m$ by $p_m-p_n$.
For a Gibbs state it equals
$(1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega})S^>_{ab}(\omega)$.
For Hermitian readouts the spectral matrix $S^>(\omega)$ is
positive semidefinite as a matrix-valued measure. The causal time
support gives upper-half-plane analyticity of the transform under
the usual convergence assumptions. Kramers–Kronig relations link
its real and imaginary parts, with subtractions when growth requires
them. A detailed-balance test presumes a specified physical time
and calibration. At zero frequency the commutator can lose static
weight. In a tracial finite state all these retarded expectations
vanish for every $H$; unsymmetrized correlations can still carry
dynamical data.

Conservation is a further consistency condition. For an identified
charge $Q$ with $[H,Q]=0$, its readout is stationary. Expectation
values of commutators with a neutral perturbation vanish in the
charge channel. A local continuity law with defined densities and
currents yields linked response functions, including contact terms
from differentiating the retarded step. Such Ward identities test
a proposed identification of the current and the symmetry. Current
definitions, gravitational gauge conditions and allowed interactions
remain separate inputs.

| Known finite full matrix algebra, operator labels and tomographically spanning preparations | Every $[A_a(t),B]$ as an operator from its expectations. ## Causality and continuum limitations

A vanishing expected commutator can come from a chosen state,
polarization, selection rule or inaccessible channel. Relativistic
microcausality is an operator commutation property for spacelike
regions. In finite analytic dynamics even a genuine coupling's
signal can start at high Taylor order, look zero at finite
precision and cross different thresholds. If the positive-time
analytic response of a finite normal-mode system vanishes exactly
on an interval, that component vanishes identically. A strict
retarded cone in a local hyperbolic field theory is a different kind
of statement. Lieb–Robinson bounds can yield approximate cones for
specified local dynamics with interaction and norm conditions, using
a supplied interaction metric. [Lieb and Robinson](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01645779)

A prescribed nearest-neighbor chain of spacing $a$ with spring
coefficient $c^2/a^2$ and pin $\omega_0^2$ gives
$\omega^2(k)=\omega_0^2+4c^2 a^{-2}\sin^2(ka/2)$.
At $|ka|\le1$ its difference from
$\omega_0^2+c^2k^2$ is at most $c^2a^2 k^4/12$, by the cosine
Taylor bound. This controlled long-wavelength wave equation is
a direct use of the chosen locality, spacing and scaling. The
four-site obstruction has no continuum limit of this kind built
into it. A relativistic field characteristic structure, a shared
cone across interactions, and gravitational equations are separate
claims. Retarded susceptibilities and conservation alone supply
none of the multiplicity of assumptions in, for example,
Weinberg's soft-graviton result. [Weinberg](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049)

## Exact continuum check under conformal covariance

In four dimensions let $P_g=\Box_g-R[g]/6$ act on a free
massless scalar with the corresponding sign convention for
curvature. For a smooth positive $\Omega$,
$\tilde g=\Omega^2 g$ has the same causal order and
$$
P_{\tilde g}(\Omega^{-1}\phi)=\Omega^{-3}P_g\phi .
$$
On globally hyperbolic backgrounds the retarded inverse with
zero past data is unique. Thus for scalar sources
$$
G_{\tilde g}^{\rm ret} f
 =\Omega^{-1}G_g^{\rm ret}(\Omega^{3} f).
$$
Relative to the appropriate invariant volume in each background,
the bidistribution kernels have a factor $\Omega^{-1}(x)$ at the
receiver and $\Omega^{-1}(y)$ at the source. Take the same open
apparatus region $U$ in both and let $\Omega=1$ throughout $U$.
Smear both arguments in $U$. The complete restricted retarded
kernels agree with the same source calibration and proper clocks
on $U$. This holds even for two probes whose intervening
causal diamond includes the support of the conformal deformation.
The free field commutator is fixed by its causal propagator.
Corresponding quasifree states can also be compared by transport
under the conformal field map. [Pinamonti](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-009-0780-x)

For example, take flat four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime and a
smooth $\Omega=1+\varepsilon b>0$ with a compact bump $b$ outside
$U$ and $\Box_\eta b$ nonzero somewhere. Then
$R[\Omega^2\eta]=-6\Omega^{-3}\Box_\eta\Omega$ is nonzero somewhere.
The geometries are inequivalent under isometry despite identical
calibrated response data in this scalar sector. This is a
kinematic comparison on prescribed Lorentzian metrics. A theory
that fixes allowed metrics by matter sources and gravitational
field equations supplies a narrower admissible set. A calibrated
massive or nonconformally coupled probe, additional geometric
measurements, or independent boundary data can break the particular
response invariance. The result concerns how much the specified
observables determine even in a known field class.
