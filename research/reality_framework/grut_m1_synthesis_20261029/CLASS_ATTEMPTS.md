# CLASS power-spectrum attempts and convergence limits

The user requested a matter-power prediction for the inherited two-component population. We built CLASS v3.4.1 from source commit `814ce59c380a5cccd0bed1747a384ff7bfc1f9cf` and ran matched cold and cold-plus-non-cold linear matter spectra. The code creates parameter files from `code/class_power.py`; spectra and summary values are saved as `class_spectra.npz` and `class_results.json`. The tested redshift is z=0, `h=.674`, `omega_b=.0224`, total dark `omega=.12`, `n_s=.965`, `A_s=2.1e-9`, and `N_ur=3.044`. The warm-like fraction is 0.0697373 of the cold-plus-held mass. The noncold phase-space shape is extrapolated to early times; M1 sources, collisions, and core-gas perturbation correlations are not included.

`psd_96.dat` is generated from the saved 96-bin positive population by a lognormal smoothing kernel of width 0.18, including its numerical floor. This additional one-point distribution ansatz increases `<q²>` by 6.7%. `psd_maxwell96.dat` is a separate smooth Maxwell-like control with exactly matched node `<q²>`; its mean q differs by 3.45%. Neither ansatz derives primordial or angular correlations. We checked a discrete integration-by-parts identity involving the distribution derivative in `psd_audit.json`: with qmax=4 the actual-spectrum quadrature gives 1.01334, 0.99488 and 1.06240 for 30, 60 and 120 nodes respectively, versus the smooth-control value 0.99989. A finite-q endpoint also matters. These diagnostics indicate quadrature sensitivity; they do not prove the source of every solver residual.

For the actual-spectrum setup using the Runge–Kutta evolver and the ordinary fluid switch, 30, 60 and 120 nodes produce `ΔP/P` in percent at `k=1 h/Mpc` of `+0.12916`, `−0.04908`, and `+0.60396`. These do not converge and some have the wrong sign for an intended suppression. A moment-matched Maxwell-like 30-node RK control gives, as percentages, `−0.000812`, `−0.000713`, `−0.000391`, and `−0.000264` at k=`0.1, 1, 10, 20 h/Mpc`; with the default NDF solver the corresponding values are `+0.00372`, `+0.00404`, `−0.01037`, and `+0.02690`. These are small relative to the proposed 8–12%, but the residual solver differences are larger than the putative fine signal. These are exploratory controls, not a quantitative physical exclusion.

Other recorded attempts:

* The narrow PSD with the default NDF and 30 manual nodes hit a step-size failure. A forced switch at `kτ=1` hit an approximation-switch coincidence. Forcing it much earlier, at `kτ=.1`, gave apparently small 30/60-node differences, but that early fluid closure is an uncontrolled approximation and is not a convergence cure.
* A tighter-tolerance NDF Maxwell run encountered a singular sparse-LU failure. The high-k (`kmax=1000/Mpc`) RK runs and two fully automatic momentum setups were interrupted after resource/time pressure; there is no resulting spectrum. No successful output from those attempts is claimed.
* kmax=20/Mpc runs store samples to roughly 33 `h/Mpc`; the 120-node actual-spectrum control was limited to kmax=2/Mpc and its direct comparison to about 3.3 `h/Mpc`.

The conditional free-streaming length is much shorter than Lyman-α scales, which motivates skepticism about the proposed large step. But **no converged M1 power suppression is reported**: the recorded CLASS setups neglect formation and interactions, and the actual-spectrum momentum integration fails the tests above. The appropriate next step is a collision-aware perturbation calculation with independent tests of momentum discretization and the population source.
