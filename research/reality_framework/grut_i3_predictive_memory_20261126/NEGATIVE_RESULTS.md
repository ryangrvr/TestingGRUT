# Negative results and scope limits

- Acquiring a strong record is insufficient. In the passive arm the
  acquired match probability is .949099 while final bond probability
  is .478354. Disabled sensing and a shuffled actuator also give
  .478354. The shuffled record remains reconstructible from $A,C$
  jointly at delivery; the control depends on screening $C$.
  Inversion retains the actuator–source mutual information of the
  predictive arm while falling to .196801.
- The .70 target fails in 16 of 30 delay/switching grid points and
  in the severe noise row. At $q=.32$ and $q=1$ every registered
  delay misses. The severe row's .557014 is below even its relaxed
  .594553 open-loop comparator. Endpoint correlations can be created
  by selective kinetics; the disabled arm has .290519 nats of
  final reagent–device mutual information with zero initial action
  information.
- Memory buys reliability at a fixed work cap. The disabled and
  passive physical arms have lower reset cost. A claim of a universal
  reduction in work or a minimum work across other devices has no
  support here.
- A free present $B$ cue gives primary reliability .839075 without
  an upstream record. Necessity is relative to the causal interface.
  Readable failed attempts, control-dependent delays, earlier actuator
  programming, a second full-rate port, or source backaction require
  a different comparator or a recalculated leakage bound.
- The scaling guarantee uses an independently prepared port, fuel,
  and source for each compartment. Shared environmental noise or a
  fixed total stock could invalidate it. The finite calculation gives
  no collective phase transition.
- The contact clock, barrier selectivity, activated jump regime and
  initial chemical preparation are supplied. The thermal ledger sets
  a work allowance for a physical implementation. Its accuracy,
  achievable overhead and reset time are for calibration. External
  replication and direct measurements remain pending.
- Existing stochastic kinetics, value of information, and
  nonequilibrium free energy explain the theorem and all tests. The
  additional-principle claim was closed at the feasibility gate.
