# The joint result and the next empirical dependency

Claude's `a929f94` audit strengthens the finite-time preparation result and
corrects real defects in our presentation. It does not establish that the entire
surviving gravity-only family is observationally equivalent to cold dark matter.
The correct decision remains: **identified inputs and calculations are missing
before Candidate 1 can make a fixed, discriminating empirical prediction.**

This is a retrospective feasibility analysis. No observed residual was fitted,
no additional action term was adopted, and no new observational prediction was
frozen. Our original packets remain byte-for-byte unchanged.

## 1. What the audit establishes

Claude reports an archive reader reproducing twelve recorded rows, nine exact
reruns of our frozen integrator, and two newly completed larger-box seeds. The
two new seeds support finite-time two-sign formation for those preparations;
they remain Claude's executions, not retroactive additions to our twelve.
The same integrator's bitwise replay is reproduction, not a new discretization

The quasi-linear zero-mode calculation and controlled changes to X and infrared
support explain why the sparse seed can select a vacuum. This agrees with our
original warning that initial cross-correlation alone does not determine the
eventual sign. Dense short-range states and a globally specified 64-mode state
are different preparation families. Neither finite family fixes primordial
correlations or a cosmological probability.

We independently reread all twelve production diagnostic CSVs here. The corrected
pressure identity holds to floating-point precision. The minority phase in main
seed 101 is transient and falls below the declared 10% formation criterion;
"no formation" without that qualification is misleading. Accepted corrections,
including the missing gradient term, shared coefficients, winding-mask limits

## 2. Why the observability conclusion is too broad

Claude's own `audit/v_reach/NOTES.md`, V2–V5 and verdict 8, narrows the uniform
unobservability claim. It retains, conditionally:

- late biased-network debris with an estimated free-streaming length as large
  as 0.28 Mpc in a selected corner;
- a PTA-band gravitational-wave tail whose estimated distance from uncertain
  future sensitivities is not a uniform large margin;
- a causal mass ceiling in the microlensing range, while the much smaller
  per-fastest-cell mass is an estimate without a merger calculation.

These are **not detections or validated reach forecasts**. Their amplitudes,
fractions, transfer functions and histories remain conditional. But they prevent
the universal claim that no current or planned observation can test any surviving
history. An unknown or uncalibrated history is not a theorem of invisibility.
Likewise, a finite scan finding no usable test would not prove exact or universal
empirical equivalence of the whole action family to CDM.

The defensible narrower statement is that the specific cold-condensate,
small-composite benchmark estimates examined are very close to collisionless
CDM in the channels estimated. A free-streaming length is not itself a
Lyman-alpha likelihood, a geometric area is not a transport cross section,
and a gravitational-wave upper envelope is not a guaranteed signal.

## 3. Scale and parameter provenance

The full action has independent parameters v and m_D0. **Correction to the
emphasis of our preliminary issue reply:** the original WORKED_REGIME already
chooses m_D0=g_X v. Thus Claude's proposed single-scale trajectory is available
without inventing that relation. We use that explicitly supplied trajectory,
fixed dimensionless couplings, and fixed physical M_Pl. It is a family of
benchmarks, not an action-derived relation or measured value of v.

Holding m_D0=100 GeV instead would be a different scan outside the classical
D=0 phi-X slice. The original worked v/M_Pl=10^-5 and the later physical
TeV cosmology are also different supplied gravitational matchings; neither is
obtained by relabelling units. Matter-loop decoupling of the visible sector is
used at the stated approximation; no all-orders theorem about gravitational
EFT portals is asserted here.

The unchanged action conserves total X charge and supplies neither its primordial
value nor a net-charge producer. All-DM density calibrates only

    e_eff g_X v r = K,       r=Y_Q/Y_B.

Using the frozen BBN baryon calibration and baryon-mass convention, together with
the already known Planck base-Lambda-CDM value omega_c=0.120, gives
**K=5.04075 GeV**. Claude's 5.0422 differs by about 0.03%, consistent with the
rounded entropy/yield conversion. The illustrative independent-error calibration
uncertainty is 1.81%, plus a separate 1% baryon-mass convention allowance. These
are not the full physical uncertainty: Planck's inference assumes a cosmological
model and cannot be imported unchanged if the proposed sector appreciably alters
its CMB transfer functions.

Here e_eff is the late total hidden nonrelativistic energy per net charge divided
by m_X. It must include unbound charge, neutral massive relics and retained
binding energy where relevant. It is not automatically the energy/charge of
identified cores. Relativistic components require separate accounting. Matching
this density is calibration, not an abundance prediction.

## 4. What the scale calculation actually gives

For the archived stationary profile, Q=39943.846, e=0.91108652 and R90bar=5.103432.
At fixed shape, E is proportional to v, R to 1/v, and the **contact proxy**
pi(2 R90)^2/E is proportional to v^-3. We obtain:

| Physical v | Mass | R90 | Contact proxy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1000 GeV | 3.63923e6 GeV | 1.00704e-15 cm | 1.96440e-12 cm²/g |
| 0.1 GeV | 363.923 GeV | 1.00704e-11 cm | 1.96440 cm²/g |

For this profile the 0.1 and 1 cm²/g proxy contours occur at v=0.26982 and
0.12524 GeV. This supports examining a sub-GeV scale, **not** claiming a SIDM
prediction or observationally allowed point. R90 contains 90% of the energy;
it is not a hard interaction boundary or a cross-section upper bound.

There is also a serious cosmological rescaling issue. At fixed e_eff and constant
visible g_* values, the all-DM calibration gives r proportional to 1/v, the
charge-restoration crossing temperature proportional to v^(4/3), and H/m_X
proportional to v^(5/3). At v=0.1 GeV the illustrative prototype-only calibration
gives r=553.27 and H/m_X about 8.2e-16 to 1.2e-15 over the declared constant-g
sensitivity bracket. These are diagnostics, not a precise Standard Model thermal
history or a computed relic composition. Our accelerated expanding run had
H/m_X about 0.01575. Its history cannot be transferred to this physical scale by
changing the units.

## 5. A population mean cannot supply the observable

Even under a deliberately simplified constant-density, thin-wall contact model,
the number-averaged collision count depends on moments of Q^(1/3), Q^(2/3) and Q,
and a mass-weighted count needs additional moments. Neither is fixed by the
charge-weighted mean Q.

A constructive diagnostic uses two populations with exactly the same
charge-weighted mean Q=10^6. One is monodisperse. The other has Q=2e4 and 1e7,
with number fraction 0.00021773 in the larger charge. The latter's normalized
number-averaged collision coefficient is **3.3504 times** the former's; its
mass-weighted collision-count coefficient is **9.5276 times** the former's.
These are analytic proxy diagnostics, not simulated Candidate 1 populations or
actual heat-transport rates.

As a separate descriptive check, applying a quoted Q>=17660 charge cut to the
archived fine-grid 10% endpoint retains fifteen of thirty-nine components. Under
the same simplified proxy its two coefficients differ by factors 1.102 and 1.590
from substituting the retained sample's charge-weighted mean. Changing threshold
or grid changes the sample; at 2% the fine result is one connected network above
the cut. Passing a charge cut does not identify a relaxed, bound soliton. These
old endpoint samples remain neither a production distribution nor a relic mass
function.

## 6. Calibration rank and a constructive way forward

In the optimistic monodisperse thin-wall limit, density constrains v r and the
contact proxy constrains v^-3 Q^-1/3. Their logarithmic calibration matrix has
rank two for three unknowns (v,Q,r), with null direction (1,-9,-1). Along it,
density and the proxy stay fixed while mass varies as the eighth inverse power
of the scale change. This limiting example demonstrates why two adjusted
combinations do not automatically fix an independent third observable. It is
not a degeneracy theorem for the complete finite-Q model.

There is nevertheless a useful candidate chain. For a **fixed dimensionless
population and state ensemble**, the classical two-body collision kernel has
cross sections proportional to v^-2. Dividing by mass gives v^-3. Ratios of
the same appropriate population/velocity-averaged transport response at two
speeds therefore cancel the physical scale. One response amplitude could
calibrate v while another tests the predicted speed dependence. Density would
calibrate r only after the full energy accounting. This does not require a new
visible portal, and it does not identify the fields uniquely among other SIDM
models.

However, geometry alone cannot calculate this ratio. The collision outcomes
depend on Q1,Q2, relative phase, impact parameter and relative speed; merger,
re-splitting, free charge and radiation cannot be represented by an elastic
area. The finite-time pair experiment already makes that concern concrete.
For the archived prototype, k R90 is about 3.10 at 10 km/s and 9.29 at 30 km/s;
it is independent of v. Lowering v does not by itself improve the semiclassical
scattering limit. At 30 km/s one R90 traversal takes about 51000/m_X, far
beyond the old isolated-object window. Production and collision costs must be
estimated before promising a broad numerical scan.
