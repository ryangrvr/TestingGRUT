## Selected target

Predict the change in a specified, halo-matched interaction response between two
velocity ranges after calibrating one amplitude. Observable endpoints would be
stellar line-of-sight velocity-dispersion profiles and strong-lensing enclosed
masses in specified relaxed groups/clusters, through an explicit baryon/halo
forward model. A cross-section ratio alone is an intermediate quantity, not the
measured observable. The gravity-only sector can affect halo structure through
its own nongravitational self-interactions.

The scale-independent ratio in DERIVATION §1 is the constructive possibility.
It applies to a fixed dimensionless population and ensemble, not all histories.
A measured deviation could favor an interacting dark component over a specified
CDM baseline without identifying Candidate 1 uniquely. Other composite or
velocity-dependent SIDM models are necessary competitors.

## Gate A: define the model and the physical preparation

- Use the existing fixed-coupling, m_D0=g_X v trajectory and state the physical
  M_Pl, matching/renormalization order, vacuum-energy calibration and geometry.
- Specify charge yield provenance, supply time/form, temperature history,
  perturbation auto- and cross-spectra, vacuum sign, field velocities, neutral
  components and D-state assumptions. Exact U(1) conservation cannot itself
  generate a nonzero total charge from zero.
- State which parameters will be independently calibrated. It is legitimate
  to calibrate v and a yield with distinct observations, provided neither is
  then advertised as an independent prediction of those same observations.
- Changing initial data is a new preparation version. Adding a producer,
  inflaton interaction or portal is an action extension requiring its own
  version, parameter list and owner approval before becoming the empirical target.

## Gate B: calculate a bounded collision pilot first

Start from the already archived stationary prototype, not an assumed relic mass
function. Freeze pilot numerical settings and error criteria before outcomes.
Compute a limited set of relative speeds, phases and impact parameters, beginning
at accessible speeds and estimating the cost of reaching halo speeds. Include
off-axis 3D collisions, a separated noninteracting control, charge-conjugate/mirror
controls as applicable, and non-overlapping boundary/domain controls. Demonstrate
that initial separation and tail interactions do not choose the outcome.

Track the complete fields: outgoing charges/energies, unresolved material,
radiation, core identities, fusion and splitting, scattering angles where a
two-core channel exists, and losses across the domain. Do not infer elastic
transport from a core radius or a visually surviving pair. Average over a
physically justified relative-phase distribution; uniform phase is an assumption,
not a consequence of U(1) conservation.

Refine dt and dx independently, enlarge the box/duration and vary operational
core definitions. Estimate errors in the **required kernel/response**, not just
energy conservation. A convergence failure or a cost ceiling is a reportable
result. Low-speed capture, long-lived bound pairs or many-body outcomes may
invalidate an instantaneous binary elastic halo model. Assess kR and quantum,
gravity and D effects at the claimed order rather than assuming them absent.

Proceed only if this pilot supplies or bounds a response with a plausible
observable magnitude. A constant geometric-area ratio is not that calculation.

## Gate C: only then specify the needed population calculation

The output must be conditional on the full Gate A history, including H/m_X and
the spectrum, not just n/n_c. Demonstrate a controlled slow-expansion limit or
match the physical H/m_X; relabelling accelerated units is insufficient. Test
resolution, box size, duration, independent realizations, spectral infrared/UV
support and initial cross-correlations. A connected density network is not an
object population. Freeze energy/charge budgets for material inside cores and
outside them, and report identification and survival uncertainties.

Stop when the **observable-weighted distribution and outcome kernels**, rather
than an arbitrary count or charge-weighted mean, meet the numerical uncertainty
budget. Determine whether later merging, evaporation, ambient gas and charge loss
matter to the selected epochs. Finite isolated-object survival cannot replace
this calculation or establish a cosmological lifetime.

## Gate D: freeze an actual independent observational test

Before a test comparison, publish an immutable protocol containing numerical
parameters and provenance, state/population and collision models, named calibration
and test samples with disjoint objects and exposure declarations, measured
observable/estimator, nuisance priors, likelihood/statistic, competing models,
convergence/error requirements, and explicit rejection/support rules. Include
selection, baryonic, halo-history and dataset-covariance uncertainties; calibrating
and testing similar objects does not automatically make their systematics independent.

The Sagunski et al. (2021) public group/cluster sample is a feasibility example
whose summary results are **already exposed**. It is not an unused held-out
sample in this session. Neither its cluster sample nor Planck may be silently
relabelled blinded. Use a genuinely unused, named sample or a future measurement
for a confirmatory test, or label an existing-data analysis retrospective.

No such unused test sample or independently fixed complete parameter set exists
in this packet. Therefore the status of the observational freeze is
**NOT READY**, and no Candidate 1 comparison statistic is computed.

## Stop rules and next unresolved dependency

Stop honestly if the population/history cannot be independently fixed or bounded
tightly enough, the collision/halo mapping fails, all calculated signals are
inaccessible within the declared scope, or numerical cost prevents the required
convergence. Report a conditional constraint or an inability to predict; do not
infer universal CDM equivalence from those failures.

The immediate unresolved dependency is a defined physical ensemble and a bounded
collision-response pilot. The theoretical work can proceed analytically while
Claude builds a conditional scale map. No need for an arbitrary new mass, yield
or portal to create an apparent successful prediction.
