## Exact limit on a probe-only comparison

The following is a conventional quantum measurement calculation, not a new
GRUT restriction. Let the source have orthogonal branches L and R. Compare

\[
\rho_c=|+\rangle\langle+|,\quad
|+\rangle=(|L\rangle+|R\rangle)/\sqrt2,\qquad
\rho_m=(|L\rangle\langle L|+|R\rangle\langle R|)/2.
\]

Both source preparations are initially independent of the same probe state
\(\sigma_P\). Matching mean mass density additionally requires negligible
off-diagonal matrix elements of the relevant mass-density operators:
\(\langle M(x)\rangle_c-\langle M(x)\rangle_m
=\operatorname{Re}\langle R|M(x)|L\rangle\).
Orthogonality alone does not establish that condition. Actual packet overlap
and preparation errors must be bounded independently.

For a branch-preserving interaction

\[
U=|L\rangle\langle L|\otimes U_L+
  |R\rangle\langle R|\otimes U_R,
\]

the joint output difference is

\[
\Delta\rho_{SP}=\tfrac12
 |L\rangle\langle R|\otimes U_L\sigma_P U_R^\dagger+
 \text{Hermitian conjugate}.
\]

Tracing over the source eliminates both terms:

\[
\boxed{\rho_{P,c}'=\rho_{P,m}'=
\tfrac12(U_L\sigma_PU_L^\dagger+U_R\sigma_PU_R^\dagger).}
\]

Every probe-only measurement has identical probabilities, including a quantum
probe's phase readout. The result also holds when P includes initially identical
probe ancillas or record devices. It does not cover source mixing, later source
recombination followed by renewed interaction, or arbitrary gravitational
dynamics. Those histories need their own operational calculation.

Thus a coherent quantum probe alone is insufficient in this declared interval.

## Information recovery is different from information destruction

\(\nu=\operatorname{Tr}(U_L\sigma_PU_R^\dagger)\). Lost local visibility can be
stored in correlations. Indeed

\[
\tfrac12\|\rho_{SP,c}'-\rho_{SP,m}'\|_1
=\tfrac12\|\rho_c-\rho_m\|_1=\tfrac12.
\]

irreversibility. The mixture preparation's retained labels and apparatus must

Conversely, successful recoherence under certified controls demonstrates

gravitational process. An effective two-body Hamiltonian does not reveal
